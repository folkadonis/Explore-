# Tamil culture guide

A reference for writers, artists and designers so everything in the game stays grounded, native and accurate to Tamil country around 1000 CE. When in doubt, check here before inventing.

---

## 1. Festivals in the game

| Festival | Tamil month (approx. Gregorian) | What happens | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thai Pongal** (4 days) | Thai (mid-January) | **Bhogi:** burn old things at dawn, children beat small drums. **Pongal:** new rice and milk boiled in a new clay pot until it boils over toward the sun; everyone shouts *"Pongalo Pongal!"* **Mattu Pongal:** cattle bathed, horns painted, garlanded; jallikattu. **Kaanum Pongal:** visiting family | Interlude I, Epilogue |
| **Masi Magam** | Masi (February–March) | Sacred bathing in temple tanks and the sea; floating lamps | Ch. 2 (lamp-boats in the tank) |
| **Chithirai ther festival** | Chithirai (April–May) | The temple chariot pulled around the car streets by the whole town | Interlude IV |
| **Aadi Perukku** | Aadi 18 (early August) | Thanksgiving to the rising rivers; mixed-rice offerings, yellow threads, floating lamps, newly-weds float their old garlands | Interlude II |
| **Karthigai Deepam** | Karthigai full moon (November–December) | Rows of clay agal lamps on every house; the great hill beacon; *"Annamalaikku arogara!"*; sokkapanai bonfire | Interlude III |
| **Thiruvathirai (Arudra Darshan)** | Margazhi (December–January) | Nataraja's cosmic dance, on the night of the red star Thiruvathirai | Ch. 4 (star floor) |
| **Margazhi kolams** | Margazhi | The biggest, most elaborate kolams of the year, drawn before dawn | Ch. 5, floor 1 |

## 2. Games, sports and arts

| Name | What it is | Rules to get right |
|---|---|---|
| **Jallikattu / eru thazhuvuthal** | Bull-embracing | Hold the hump only (not horns or tail) for a set distance; the coin bundle (*salli kaasu*) is tied to the horns; the bull is never harmed; honour goes to the owner's house when the bull is held |
| **Silambam** | Bamboo staff fighting | Staff cut to the user's brow height; footwork (*kaaladi*) comes first; spinning guards; drum-led displays |
| **Kabaddi / sadugudu** | Breath-holding team raid | One raider chants on one breath, tags defenders, returns across the line before breath runs out |
| **Uriyadi** | Blindfolded pot breaking | Pot raised and lowered by rope from rooftops; turmeric water splashed at the striker |
| **Pallanguzhi** | Mancala board game | 14 cups in two rows; cowrie shells or tamarind seeds; sowing and capturing |
| **Kolam** | Rice-flour threshold drawing | Drawn freehand by women each dawn; *sikku kolam* is a single line looping around a dot grid without lifting the hand |
| **Parai** | Frame drum | The heartbeat of festivals and processions |
| **Nadaswaram and thavil** | Double-reed pipe and barrel drum | Temple and wedding music |
| **Kummi** | Women's clapping circle dance | Festival nights |
| **Oyilattam** | Line dance with coloured scarves | Festivals and processions |
| **Therukoothu** | All-night street theatre | Epic episodes, painted faces, tall headdresses, a sung narrator |

## 3. Places and architecture

- **Kanchi** (Kanchipuram) sits on the **Vegavathi** river, with the Palar nearby. It is *not* on the Kaveri.
- **Temple tank (kulam):** stepped sides, a central pavilion (*neerazhi mandapam*). Model on the Mahamaham tank, Kumbakonam.
- **Car streets (ther veedhi):** the four wide streets around a temple, built for the chariot.
- **Kammalar streets:** artisans' quarters (metal, stone, wood, gold).
- **Vaadivaasal:** the narrow timber gate bulls run out of at jallikattu.
- **Building vocabulary:** vimana (tower over the shrine), gopuram (gateway tower), mandapam (pillared hall), adhishthana (moulded plinth), dwarapala (door guardian), kalasam (finial pot), thoranam (mango-leaf string over doors).

## 4. Food (period check)

**Use:** rice in every form, ghee, jaggery, sesame, black pepper, long pepper, ginger, turmeric, mustard seed, tamarind, coconut, plantain, jackfruit, mango, sugarcane, moong dal, cardamom, curd, honey.

**Don't use (came after 1500 CE from the Americas or with the Portuguese):** chillies, tomatoes, potatoes, cashews, peanuts, maize, papaya, pineapple. Tea and coffee are also out.

**Named dishes:** sakkarai pongal (sweet), ven pongal (savoury, pepper and ghee), kanji (rice gruel, for famine relief), puffed rice, payasam, mixed rices for Aadi Perukku (tamarind, coconut, curd, sesame, jaggery).

## 5. Clothing and ornament

- **Men:** veshti (often tied as a *kacham* for work and fighting), angavastram (upper cloth) for status, long hair in a knot (*kondai*), ear ornaments.
- **Women:** draped cotton or silk, worn without stitched blouses in this period; jasmine in braided hair; the **thali** for married women; anklets (*kolusu*) with bells.
- **Colours from natural dyes:** indigo, madder red, turmeric yellow, lac. No neon, no chemical purples.

## 7. Tone

- **Grounded:** these are working people's festivals: dust, smoke, sweat, cattle, bad jokes, children everywhere. Not postcard-perfect.
- **Respectful:** worship is shown, not mocked or made exotic. Deities are mostly off screen or veiled.
- **Avoid:** "mystical India" clichés, snake charmers, generic "Indian" music, Bollywood styling, north Indian architecture and dress, and any harm to animals.
