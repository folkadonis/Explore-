# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Chapter 5: "The Mirror Temple"

**Runtime:** about 50 minutes · **Region:** the 13 tiers of the Mirror Temple, floating apart in a violet sky · **Arc stage:** 5, the guardian
**New power:** Eclipse (darken a whole room for three seconds so every surface becomes shade; three charges, refilled at shrines)
**Final boss:** Nizhal

---

**EXT. THE FOOT OF THE MIRROR TEMPLE. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

The tower has come apart. Its thirteen tiers hang in the air one above another, slowly turning, with gaps of sky between them. Chunks of granite drift in orbit. At the summit, a violet light pulses like a heart.

At the temple doors, in stone: **KEEPER SENDAN**, Kayal's father, one hand raised toward the door as if to stop someone. ILAN stops in front of him. In his sash: Kayal's bow, the burnt letter, the Tirukkural leaf.

> **ILAN**
> I'll bring her back, Keeper.

> **NIZHAL**
> He can't hear you.

> **ILAN**
> I know.

He climbs.

> **Structure:** each floor holds **one shadow-memory of an ordinary person in Kanchi**, playing silently as you pass through it. These are the people Ilan is saving. The player can stop and watch any of them; none are required. Each memory is one Tamil life, one craft, one moment.

---

**FLOOR 1: THE THRESHOLD. (THE KOLAM AT DAWN)**

A memory: a grandmother and a small girl drawing the morning kolam at their door before sunrise, the girl's lines wobbly, the grandmother's perfect. The grandmother doesn't correct her. She just draws around the wobbles so they become part of the pattern.

> **GAMEPLAY:** climb the floating base stones. Sun and violet sky. Nothing hostile yet.

---

**FLOOR 2: THE LIT ROOM. (TEACH ECLIPSE)**

A room with no shadow at all: lit from every side by shard-light pouring through its walls. A gap you can't cross.

> **NIZHAL**
> There's no shade here.

> **ILAN**
> Then make it dark. All of it.

> **NIZHAL**
> *(a long pause)*
> I can. It'll cost me.

> **GAMEPLAY: ECLIPSE (teach)**
>
> - Hold **STEP + CUT** together: Nizhal spreads out across the whole room like ink in water. For three seconds **everything is shade**: every wall, the ceiling, the air above gaps. Step anywhere.
> - Three charges. Each use makes Nizhal visibly thinner and slower to follow, until a shrine refills him.

---

**FLOORS 3 TO 4: THE WEDDING**

A memory on a turning floor: a Tamil wedding at dawn. A pandal of plantain stems and coconut fronds, the nadaswaram at full cry, the groom tying the **thali** around the bride's neck at the exact auspicious moment while everyone throws yellow rice. Guests eat off plantain leaves in long rows.

> **GAMEPLAY:** the floor rotates, so shadows sweep around it like a clock hand. Step on the sweep.

---

**FLOOR 5: THE SEA TRADER**

A memory: a merchant back from Nagapattinam port, sunburnt, unpacking for his wife the things he brought from across the sea: camphor from Srivijaya, a Chinese porcelain bowl, a bolt of silk. Their small son is more interested in the ship's rope he brought back.

> **GAMEPLAY:** Bronze Guardians on rotating platforms. Strike from moving shade.

---

**FLOOR 6: THE FAMINE KITCHEN**

A memory, darker in tone: the drought years. A temple kitchen feeding a long queue of the hungry with rice gruel (kanji) ladled from a huge pot. Near the end of the queue, a small girl with an anklet that rings each time she shuffles forward: **VALLI**, thin now. Her father is not with her; he is at the palace, begging the old king for the granaries to be opened. The ladle reaches the bottom of the pot just before her turn.

The memory stops there. Ilan stands and looks at it for a long time.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I understood him then. Not forgave. Understood.

> **GAMEPLAY:** Anchor + Eclipse: hold a gate open with Nizhal while the room is dark. You must Anchor, then Eclipse, then cross. Nizhal grumbles that he's being asked to be in two places.

---

**FLOOR 7: THE THERUKOOTHU**

A memory at night: a village square, a **therukoothu** troupe performing the Mahabharata on bare earth by torchlight. Huge painted faces, towering headdresses, bells on their ankles, a narrator singing between scenes. The crowd sits on the ground until dawn. A small boy in the front row has fallen asleep on his father's lap: Ilan, age five, with his father.

> **ILAN**
> *(barely)*
> Appa took me. I don't remember the play. I remember the torches.

> **GAMEPLAY:** Lamp-bearer ambush around the torch-lit square. Eclipse cancels their light.

---

**FLOOR 8: THE WALL**

A memory: **SENGAN**, Ilan's father, kneeling at the foot of the Mirror Temple's outer wall, setting the lowest stone. He cuts his mason's mark into it: the crescent with a dot. Then, glancing around to make sure no one is watching, he cuts a second, tiny mark beside it: the Tamil letter **இ** (*i*), the first letter of his son's name.

ILAN kneels where his father knelt.

> **ILAN**
> He put my name in the wall.

> **NIZHAL**
> One letter. Nobody will ever see it.

> **ILAN**
> *(smiling for the first time in hours)*
> He did.

> **Collectible:** the final shadow-memory. *"Sengan, stone-carver. He carved his son's initial in the lowest stone, where no one would ever look."*

---

**FLOORS 9 TO 11: THE OPEN SKY**

No memories, just the climb. Ilan climbs the **outside** of the floating tower between drifting stones, high above the frozen city, in sky shadows thrown by rubble passing in front of the sun.

> **GAMEPLAY:** the hardest traversal in the game. Shadow from drifting rubble passes across your path on a slow rhythm; Step from moving shadow to moving shadow, with Eclipse to save a fall.

Wind for the first time since the curse. At the top of floor 11, he can hear Kayal's voice above, and Kaalavan's.

---

**FLOOR 12: THE GAUNTLET**

A long chamber that uses every rule at once: sun shafts, furnace-pulse light, mirrors, gates, Lamp-bearers, Guardians, a flooded section. The final **test** of all five powers.

> **NIZHAL**
> *(as the doors at the far end open)*
> Last chance. Turn around. I'll make it easy.

ILAN walks through the door.

---

**FLOOR 13: THE SUMMIT**

An open platform under a violet sky, ringed with broken pillars. At its centre, the **Mirror**, rebuilt from shards in its bronze lotus cradle, cracks still glowing.

**KAYAL** is bound to a pillar by ropes of shadow. **KAALAVAN** stands before the Mirror with all his shards orbiting him. The anklet is on his wrist.

From the glass, he has pulled out a small shadow: a little girl, flat and grey, standing at the Mirror's foot. **VALLI's shadow.** She is six again.

> **KAALAVAN**
> *(kneeling to her level, arms open)*
> Valli. It's Appa. Come.

The small shadow takes one step back.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Valli-ma. It's me.

The shadow looks past him, at the whole frozen city stretched out below, at all the stone people. Then back at her father. She steps back into the Mirror's edge.

> **ILAN**
> *(from the stairhead)*
> She's afraid of you.

> **KAALAVAN**
> *(not turning)*
> I did this for her.

> **ILAN**
> You did it to her. To everyone she knew.

A very long silence. KAALAVAN looks at his own hands, at the shards orbiting them. Slowly he unties the **anklet** from his wrist. He holds it up. It rings, once.

The small shadow turns at the sound.

> **KAALAVAN**
> *(barely audible)*
> If you're ever lost. I'd hear you. That's what I promised.

He sets the anklet down at the Mirror's foot. The small shadow kneels, picks it up, and walks back into the glass, ringing.

His shards fall out of the air and clatter on the stone. KAALAVAN turns grey from the feet up, at last, like everyone else in Kanchi, kneeling, his hand still out.

ILAN cuts KAYAL free with his blade. She drops into his arms.

> **KAYAL**
> You came back.

> **ILAN**
> I brought your bow.

Then the shadows on the summit begin to move toward one place, the way water finds a drain. Toward Ilan. Toward the shadow at his feet.

---

**FLOOR 13: THE SUMMIT. CONTINUOUS. (THE FINAL FIGHT)**

NIZHAL stands up out of the floor and keeps standing up. The fallen shards fly to him and sink into him. He fills out until he isn't a flat thing anymore: a man of black smoke with red eyes, exactly Ilan's height, wearing Ilan's face like a carving.

> **NIZHAL**
> If you mend it, I go back into the glass. I go back to being nothing.

> **NIZHAL**
> Just like you're afraid of. You know exactly how it feels.

> **ILAN**
> I do.

> **NIZHAL**
> Then don't.

> **BOSS: NIZHAL (3 phases)**
>
> - **Phase 1: shadow against shadow.** Nizhal has every power Ilan has: he Steps, Anchors, Strikes and Bends light. Every shadow on the summit is his. Mirror his moves; he's strongest in shade. Strike him at the moment he Anchors (when he splits in two and each half is weak).
> - **Phase 2: in full light.** KAYAL runs the ring of pillars lighting the old summit torches with flint, one by one. The summit floods with firelight; **all shade vanishes**. Neither of them has powers now. Ilan fights a smoke-copy of himself with his short blade as a plain man: a stamina duel, slow and exhausting. The music drops to a single parai drum.
>   KAYAL (lighting the last torch): "Fight him like the captain taught you. *Feet first.*" (the silambam footwork from Interlude II comes back as a timing aid)
> - **Phase 3: letting go.** Nizhal, beaten, on his knees, shrinking back to a flat shadow.
>   > **NIZHAL:** Please. I'll be quiet. I'll be your shadow. You could keep me. Nobody would know.
>
>   The power bar is full. The button prompt is not *Strike*. It is **RELEASE**. Holding it drains the power bar to zero, permanently, while Nizhal sinks back into the Mirror's glass. If the player waits, Nizhal slowly recovers and repeats the line. The game waits for as long as it takes.

NIZHAL sinks into the glass. At the last moment, his eyes go violet again.

> **NIZHAL**
> *(Ilan's voice, gentle)*
> ...You're welcome.

He's gone. ILAN has no shadow. The sun, still stuck at noon, throws Kayal's shadow on the stone and none of his.

---

**FLOOR 13: THE SUMMIT. CONTINUOUS. (THE LEDGER)**

ILAN holds the last shard. One crack left in the Mirror.

> **KAYAL**
> Wait.

She pulls her palm-leaf satchel round, takes out a blank leaf and her bronze stylus, and kneels on the stone.

> **KAYAL**
> Tell me your name. All of it.

> **ILAN**
> You know my name.

> **KAYAL**
> I won't. In a minute. *(stylus ready)* Your name.

> **ILAN**
> Ilanthiraiyan. Son of Sengan, stone-carver.

She cuts it into the leaf, fast and neat. Then, beneath it, without asking, a second line. Insert:

*"He casts no shadow now. If you are reading this, you knew him. Believe it."*

She ties the leaf into the keeper's ledger with red thread.

> **KAYAL**
> The Mirror takes memory. It doesn't take ink.

> **ILAN**
> Kayal...

> **KAYAL**
> Go on, carver's son.

He places the last shard.

---

**EXT. KANCHI. CONTINUOUS. (THE MENDING)**

The crack closes with the sound of a temple bell rung once. Light pours out of the Mirror, not violet but plain warm daylight. The tiers of the tower drift back down onto one another and lock.

Across the city, every shadow falls back to the ground, finds its person, and stitches itself to their feet.

The sun unsticks. It slides **backwards** across the sky, through morning, back to the low gold sun of the coronation day, and the whole city runs backwards with it: thrown rice flying back into hands, the stone pigeons rising and un-shattering, Ponni's bell swinging back.

The last thing to go is Kayal, kneeling on the summit with the ledger, looking at Ilan, as the light takes them both.

1. "No. Kayal was waiting. I got up."

3. *(after any death in the Nizhal fight)* "He was always stronger in the dark. So I went to the light."

## Accuracy notes

- **Nagapattinam** was the Chola empire's great port; Chola trade and naval contact with **Srivijaya** (Sumatra) and Song China is well documented. Camphor and Chinese ceramics are period-correct imports.
- **Therukoothu** is the Tamil street theatre tradition (all-night performances of epic episodes, painted faces, large headdresses). Its roots are old; showing it in the 11th century is a reasonable fiction.
- **The thali-tying** at an auspicious moment, the nadaswaram, the pandal of banana plants and coconut fronds, and the plantain-leaf meal are all core to Tamil weddings.
- **இ** is the first letter of Ilan's name in Tamil script.
- Famine-relief kitchens run by temples are recorded in Chola inscriptions.
