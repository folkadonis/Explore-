# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Chapter 1: "The Coronation of Ash"

**Runtime:** about 35 minutes · **Region:** Mirror Temple outer face, Royal Palace terraces · **Arc stage:** 1, the proud boy
**New power:** Shadow Step · **Set piece:** escape from the coronation hall
**Playable slice:** `prototype/index.html` covers scenes 6 to 12.

---

**EXT. MIRROR TEMPLE, OUTER FACE. LATE MORNING.**

Low angle, wide lens (24mm), looking straight up the tower's north face. Thirteen tiers of black granite climb into a sky already bleaching toward noon. Halfway up, small against all that stone, is ILAN.

The tower is a climbing problem built by masons. Every tier has a cornice to hang from and a row of miniature shrines to haul up onto. The stone is warm under his palms.

> **GAMEPLAY: THE NORTH FACE (climb, about 5 min)**
>
> - Shimmy along cornices; pull up onto kuta shrine roofs; jump between pilasters.
> - Each tier has **mason's marks** cut into its stones: small signs each mason used to claim his work (a fish, a lamp, a conch). On tier 9, Ilan finds a **crescent with a dot** and stops.
>
> > **ILAN**
> > *(touching it)*
> > Appa.
>
> - Optional: hold the interact button to rest a hand on the mark. No reward; Nizhal will bring it up in Ch. 5.

---

**EXT. MIRROR TEMPLE, TIER 13, MAYAN'S DOOR. CONTINUOUS.**

A ledge barely two feet wide. Wind. Far below, the coronation hymn drifts up, thin as a thread.

The door is a stone disc four metres across, set flush into the wall, carved in three concentric rings. The outer ring shows elephants walking, the middle ring dancers, the inner ring stars. Each ring also carries one mason's mark, at a different angle.

Above the door, in Grantha letters worn soft:

*"Whose hands built me?"*

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Mayan's door. Every child in Kanchi knew the riddle.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Only one of us knew the answer.

> **GAMEPLAY: MAYAN'S DOOR (rotation puzzle, about 3 min)**
>
> - Push each ring with Ilan's shoulder to turn it. The rings grind and dust sifts down.
> - Each ring carries three different mason's marks. The solution is to line up **the crescent with a dot**, his father's mark, on all three rings at the top of the door.
> - Wrong alignments are allowed and reset with a heavy *thunk*. No timer.
> - On success: a long exhale of air from inside, cold enough to fog. The disc rolls into the wall.

---

**INT. MIRROR CHAMBER. CONTINUOUS.**

Darkness, then violet light, low and steady.

A square chamber of polished black granite. The walls are so smooth they show Ilan back to himself in four directions. In the centre, cradled in a bronze cage shaped like a lotus bud, stands the **NIZHAL KANNADI**: a disc of black glass taller than a man, its edge bound in gold.

Inside the glass, shadows move. Not reflections: shadows, with the colour drained out. A woman winnowing rice, the grain falling in a slow curtain. Two boys flying a kite from a rooftop. A soldier sitting on a step, head in his hands. Thousands of them, layered, moving at the speed of memory.

ILAN steps closer. His breath fogs the glass, and it doesn't clear.

In the nearest layer, a man kneels at a wall with a chisel and mallet. Square shoulders. Grey at the temples. The crescent-and-dot is cut on the stone in front of him.

> **ILAN**
> *(barely audible)*
> ...Appa?

The shadow-man doesn't look up. He keeps working, as he always did.

Ilan reaches toward the glass. Stops. Lifts the whole cradle instead. It's lighter than it looks, which is wrong.

> **KAALAVAN (O.S.)**
> Don't look too long. It looks back.

---

**INT. MIRROR CHAMBER. CONTINUOUS.**

KAALAVAN stands in the doorway, breathing hard from the climb, his rod of office in one hand. Behind him the sky is hot white.

> **ILAN**
> Minister. I have it. I'll carry it down myself.

> **KAALAVAN**
> You've carried enough today.

He walks forward slowly, the way you walk toward a nervous animal. His eyes never leave the glass. In it, near the bottom, a small girl's shadow sits with her knees drawn up.

> **KAALAVAN**
> *(to the glass, not to Ilan)*
> There you are.

> **ILAN**
> Who is that?

Kaalavan raises the rod. For a moment Ilan thinks it's a blessing.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Forgive me, boy. It was always going to be someone.

He brings the rod down on the Mirror's face.

---

**INT. MIRROR CHAMBER. CONTINUOUS. (SLOW MOTION)**

The sound is wrong: a deep, falling note like ice cracking across a river, played backwards.

A white fracture races across the glass. The Mirror splits into a dozen shards that turn slowly in the air. One spins toward Ilan's raised arm and **embeds itself in his bronze armband**, sinking in like a stone dropped into water.

From the broken frame, a ring of violet-black darkness rolls outward through the walls as if they weren't there.

---

**EXT. KANCHI. CONTINUOUS. (SLOW MOTION, WIDE)**

The ring of darkness sweeps over the city at the height of a man.

It passes through the coronation courtyard and **every shadow tears loose from the ground**, peeling up like cloth in a gust. Where a shadow leaves, its owner greys from the feet up and stops.

Specific moments, each held for about a second:

- A woman throwing rice at the procession. The grains stop in the air in a fan.
- An odhuvar mid-hymn, mouth open, turning to granite.
- A temple elephant lifting its trunk, the bells at its neck tilted mid-swing. It greys into stone.
- Pigeons over the courtyard turn to stone in flight and **drop**. They shatter on the flagstones like clay pots.
- The flames of ten thousand ghee lamps stop flickering and hold their shape: small and bright, perfectly still.

The sun lurches across the sky to its highest point and stops there.

Silence. Then nothing at all, not even wind.

CUT TO BLACK.

---

**EXT. MIRROR TEMPLE, LOWER STEPS. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

FADE IN on a close-up of ILAN's face against warm stone. Eyes closed. A thread of blood at his hairline. Dust motes hang in the light above him, perfectly still.

His eyes open. He coughs. He sits up slowly and looks at the armband. The shard is in it, black and faintly violet, like a sliver of night sky.

Beside him, the stone is bare. **He has no shadow.**

Then, a metre away: a shadow, lying flat on the steps where his should be. Man-shaped. Unattached.

It sits up.

Slowly, the way someone sits up after sleeping on a hard floor, it peels itself off the stone until it's sitting cross-legged beside him. Its body is pure black with soft, smoking edges. Two eyes open in its face, violet and lit from inside.

This is **NIZHAL**. It speaks with Ilan's voice, half a tone lower, and sounds amused.

> **NIZHAL**
> Well. You've really done it this time.

ILAN scrambles back two steps on his hands.

> **ILAN**
> ...Who said that?

> **NIZHAL**
> Look down. Or don't. I'm not there anymore.

> **ILAN**
> You're my shadow.

> **NIZHAL**
> I was. Now I'm your shadow with opinions.

Nizhal stands. A beat later Ilan stands too, then frowns: Nizhal got up first.

A deep crack echoes across the city. Below them, a stretch of palace roof slumps and drops into a cloud of dust.

> **NIZHAL**
> Keep up. The palace is coming down.

> **Prototype note:** the slice starts here. Nizhal's half-beat lead when standing is the first plant for his independence (story notes, fix 4).

---

**EXT. PALACE TERRACES, FIRST GAP. CONTINUOUS.**

The upper palace terraces, granite platforms with carved plinths, joined by bridges. Most of the bridges are gone. Through the gaps, far below, the lower city sits in a warm haze: rooftops, palms, crowds of grey figures in the streets.

The terrace ends at a gap far too wide to jump. On the far side, a red silk banner with the gold tiger hangs from a crossbar. Its shadow lies on the far terrace, sharp-edged and blue-violet.

> **NIZHAL**
> Too far to jump. Not too far for me.

> **ILAN**
> What does that mean?

> **NIZHAL**
> Look at the shade over there. Want to be in it.

> **ILAN**
> That's not how anything works.

> **NIZHAL**
> It is today.

> **GAMEPLAY: SHADOW STEP (teach)**
>
> - The HUD eye lights violet when a shadow is in reach (about 9.5 m). A translucent violet **ghost of Ilan** stands where he'll land.
> - Press Step. Ilan dissolves into violet dust and re-forms inside the banner's shadow.
> - Falling here costs nothing: Ilan reappears at the edge.

On the far side, ILAN stumbles out of the Step, drops to one knee and dry-heaves.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> It felt like being folded into ink.

> **NIZHAL**
> You're welcome.

---

**EXT. PALACE TERRACES, THE ELEPHANT PROCESSION. CONTINUOUS.**

A run of broken terrace islands. On each stands a temple elephant, turned to granite mid-stride. Their gold bells and forehead plates are untouched, still bright metal: **the curse took flesh and cloth, but not metal.** The bells hang tilted, frozen mid-swing.

Each elephant's belly throws a deep pool of shade onto its terrace. Between the islands there's nothing but haze and a long drop.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> The temple elephants led every procession. Their bells stopped mid-swing.

> **NIZHAL**
> Big animals. Big shade.

> **GAMEPLAY: ELEPHANT TERRACES (practise)**
>
> - Step from belly-shade to belly-shade across three islands.
> - On the last island the shade falls short. Climb the elephant's leg and strike loose the stone parasol on its howdah; it falls and leans against the elephant, throwing a longer shadow that reaches the next ledge.
> - **Statue moment (optional):** a stone flower girl, about nine, with a basket of jasmine. In stage 1 Ilan walks past without a word. (In Ch. 5, after the shadow-memories, he'll stop here and say her name: *Malar*.)

---

**EXT. PALACE TERRACES, THE BANNER GALLERY. CONTINUOUS.**

A timber gantry spans the next gap. From its beam, on two ropes, hangs a long tiger banner, still swinging from the blast. It's the only thing in Kanchi that moves.

Its shadow sweeps back and forth across the far ledge.

> **GAMEPLAY: TIMING (practise)**
>
> - The banner's shadow is reachable only on the near end of its swing (about 40% of the cycle). Wait for the ghost to appear, then Step.
> - Stepping early isn't possible (no ghost); stepping late is. If the shadow slides away just after the Step, Ilan lands at the very edge and must run.

---

**EXT. CANOPY COURT. CONTINUOUS.**

A wide sunlit courtyard broken into three terraces. Nothing stands on them except wooden rope posts with brass finials. From each post, a rope runs to a fat roll of saffron silk lashed to the top of a pair of poles: canopies, rolled up and waiting for the noon heat.

Full sun everywhere. No shade at all. The HUD eye is dark.

> **NIZHAL**
> No shade out there.

> **ILAN**
> Then I'll make some.

> **NIZHAL**
> *(delighted)*
> Now you're thinking like me.

> **GAMEPLAY: KEY PUZZLE**
>
> - Stand by a rope post; press Cut. The rope parts and the canopy unrolls across its poles in a heavy rush of silk, dropping a long shadow onto the next terrace.
> - The second post is only reachable after crossing on the first canopy's shadow.
> - After cut 1, **ILAN (V.O.):** *"That silk was meant to shade the prince."*
> - After cut 2, **ILAN (V.O.):** *"He wouldn't miss it."*

---

**INT. CORONATION HALL. CONTINUOUS.**

A long pillared hall with a heavy granite roof. Inside it's dim, lit only by tall brass lamps whose flames stand perfectly still. Strings of jasmine hang between the pillars. Down the centre runs the coronation feast on plantain leaves: rice, ghee, payasam in brass bowls. **Steam hangs over the rice in solid, glassy curls.**

The servers are stone, frozen mid-pour. A ladle of ghee hangs above a leaf in a gold thread that doesn't fall.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> The feast was still laid out. Steam hung over the rice like glass.

ILAN slows. The shadows behind the pillars aren't lying flat. They're leaning away from the walls.

> **NIZHAL**
> *(quietly)*
> Ilan. The dark is moving.

They peel off the walls: **NIZHALARS**, the torn shadows of the people in the hall, tall and stooped, with ember-red eyes. They move slowly at first, smelling the air, then turn toward the only living body in the room.

> **NIZHAL**
> They want a body back. Yours will do.

> **NIZHAL**
> Run. Get to the sun.

> **GAMEPLAY: HALL ESCAPE (set piece)**
>
> - The whole hall floor is shade, so Ilan can Step anywhere, but Nizhalars can follow anywhere too.
> - Three Walkers wait ahead; three chase from behind at a jog (slower than Ilan's run, faster than his walk).
> - Cut knocks a Walker back. **Knock one into sunlight and it burns.** That trick returns later.
> - The roof begins to fail: stones fall ahead of Ilan, each with a dust burst and a camera shake. They're scripted near-misses, not damage.
> - Burst out into the sun. The chasers reach the threshold, smoke, and burn to ash in the light.
>
> > **ILAN (V.O.)**
> > They followed me to the doorway. The light took them there.

---

**INT. TEMPLE CLOISTER. CONTINUOUS.**

The same cloister where Kaalavan stopped him that morning. ILAN walks through it, breathing hard. Something red catches his eye at the foot of a pillar.

The keeper's letter, its red thread still knotted. Half of the palm leaf is burnt black. He unrolls what's left.

Insert: Tamil script cut into the leaf with a stylus, the scratches blackened with lamp-soot:

*"...do not let the Mirror leave the tower. The minister has asked for..."*

The rest is ash.

Ilan reads it twice. He looks back toward the Mirror Temple.

> **ILAN**
> He knew.

> **NIZHAL**
> So did the keeper. You were the only one who didn't.

Ilan pushes the leaf into his sash where it was this morning.

---

**EXT. CORONATION COURTYARD. NOON.**

The courtyard from the prologue, now perfectly silent. Three thousand people in granite. The rice still hangs in the air. The silk canopies are still rolled. A crow, stone, lies on its back on the steps.

On the mandapa: **PRINCE ADITYA**, stone, standing, both arms raised. He holds the gold crown above his own head, a hand's width short of being king. The crown is still gold. It's the brightest thing in the city.

ILAN climbs the mandapa steps slowly. The camera stays behind his shoulder (85mm, shallow focus) with Aditya's stone face sharp.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Aditya stood where the priests had left him, the crown half-raised.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> We climbed the same walls as boys. He never once looked down on me.

He stops at the top step, close enough to touch. He doesn't.

NIZHAL leans against a pillar behind him, arms folded.

> **NIZHAL**
> Nobody saw it was you. You could just walk away.

A long beat. Close on Ilan: jaw tight, eyes wet, not crying.

> **ILAN**
> I could have.

He reaches out and straightens the crown in Aditya's stone hands, a finger's width, so it sits level.

Crane up and away. The city, the stopped sun, the black tower. Far below, at the city's heart, a huge square stepwell glints green.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I didn't.

## Death and restart lines (stage 1, cocky)

1. "Wait. I'm telling it wrong."
2. "I'd never fall for that."
3. "I was the best climber in Kanchi. Ask anyone."
4. "Let me tell that part again."

## Environmental puzzle ideas (Shadow Step)

1. **The parasol.** Ceremonial stone parasols stand on tall stems at the corners of the elephant terraces. Strike the stem and a parasol topples onto the next terrace; its round canopy throws a disc of shade exactly where you need it. It teaches "change the level to make shade" before the canopy court.
2. **The rolling shade.** A frozen procession cart (a small temple car) sits on a slope with its wheels chocked. Cut the chock rope and it rolls down the terrace, dragging a moving pool of shade. Step into the shade while it moves to ride it across a long sunlit stretch.

## Lore inscriptions (Kayal reads these aloud in later chapters)

1. *"Here the procession halts. The elephants kneel to the sun."* (elephant terraces)
2. *"Whose hands built me?"* (Mayan's door)
3. *"Shade is a gift. Give it to the traveller and the stranger."* (canopy posts)
4. *"The coronation lamp is lit once. It is never put out."* (hall; the lamps are frozen, not out)
5. *"Under the palace sleep the old wells. Water remembers what stone forgets."* (exit arch; points to Ch. 2)

## Sound and music

- **Before the curse:** nadaswaram and thavil from the courtyard, crowd murmur, parrots, a distant conch. Warm, busy, full.
- **After the curse:** no wind and no birds. The score is a low drone tuned to the tonic and fifth, with almost nothing on top. The only ambient sounds are ones Ilan makes: footsteps, breath, cloth.
- **Shadow Step:** a reversed breath and a soft ink-in-water rush.
- **Nizhalars:** a dry whisper of many voices saying nothing intelligible, pitched down.
- **Hall chase:** the drone climbs a semitone every 20 seconds; thavil hits on each falling stone.
- **Aditya:** silence for the first two lines, then a single sustained veena note under "I didn't."
