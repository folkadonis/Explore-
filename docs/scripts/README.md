# Story map

The whole game in order: five chapters, with a Tamil festival between each. Every festival interlude is a **playable memory**. The first is Ilan's own, told by him as narrator; the rest are held inside the Mirror shards he recovers, so each recovered shard opens someone else's festival day.

Each interlude does three jobs: it teaches a skill the next chapter needs, it moves one character's story forward, and it shows the living city that the curse froze.

## How the interludes carry the story

- **Interlude I (Ilan):** he held the palace bull and the honour went to the palace. Only Aditya said his name. *Plants:* "being seen", and the friendship the end of Chapter 1 depends on.
- **Interlude II (Kayal):** her father alive and teasing her; she and Ilan met as children and both forgot. *Plants:* her fear of losing her father's lore, and the silambam footwork that wins the final fight.
- **Interlude III (Ponni):** Kaalavan as a loving father, and Valli's anklet. *Plants:* why the villain does what he does, and the anklet's payoff at the summit.
- **Interlude IV (Aditya):** the prince always saw Ilan. Nobody moves the chariot alone. *Plants:* Ilan's reason to mend the Mirror for its own sake.
- **Epilogue:** Ilan, unseen, is the one who says another boy's name at the jallikattu. The arc closes on the same custom that opened it.

## Running images

- **Fathers' jokes:** "Lamps can't be proud." / "Hills can't be early." Sendan and Kaalavan say the same kind of thing to their daughters. Never point it out.
- **"Ask anyone":** Ilan's catchphrase, cocky in Chapter 1, kind by the epilogue.
- **The parai drum:** the jallikattu beat returns as the only music in the final fight's second phase.
- **Ink outlasts memory:** the burnt letter (Ch. 1–2), the Tirukkural leaf (Ch. 4), the keeper's ledger (Ch. 5, epilogue).
