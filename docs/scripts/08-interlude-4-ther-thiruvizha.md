# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Interlude IV: "Ther Thiruvizha" (The Chariot Festival)

**Placement:** between Chapter 4 and Chapter 5 · **Runtime:** about 15 minutes
**Whose memory:** Aditya's, held in the observatory shard (he studied the stars there as a boy)
**Festival:** the annual temple chariot festival in the month of Chithirai (April–May), when the deity is taken around the four great streets on a huge carved wooden chariot pulled by the whole city
**Events played:** **uriyadi** (breaking the hanging pot blindfolded), **oyilattam** dancing with the crowd, and **vadam pidithal**, pulling the chariot rope together
**Teaches:** that one person can't move it and a city can. Chapter 5's final climb is built on everyone Ilan has learned to see.
**Arc beat:** Aditya, through his own eyes. Ilan is at the edges of every frame of the prince's memory: in it, Ilan was always seen.

---

**EXT. THE OBSERVATORY. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

The chanting grows. The star-floor dissolves into dust and sunlight.

---

**EXT. THE FOUR CAR STREETS (THER VEEDHI), KANCHI. MORNING. (TWELVE YEARS EARLIER)**

The widest streets in the city, laid out in a square around the great temple, made wide enough for one thing only. In the morning haze stands **THE THER**: the temple chariot, five storeys of carved dark wood on six solid wheels taller than a man. Every panel is carved: gods, dancers, elephants, lovers, battles. On top, a tiered canopy of coloured cloth and a gold kalasam. The deity sits inside, hidden by silk.

Tied to the front axle, laid out along the street for two hundred metres, are four ropes as thick as a man's leg: the **vadam**.

The crowd is the whole city, and they're waiting.

**ADITYA (8)** stands on the palace platform beside the chariot, in silk too heavy for him, his tutor's hand on his shoulder. He isn't looking at the chariot. He's looking down at the crowd, searching.

> **ADITYA (V.O.)**
> *(the memory's own voice: young, earnest)*
> Every year I looked for him. The carver's boy. He was always somewhere.

He finds him: **ILAN (8)**, hanging off a lamp-post at the front of the rope, waving both arms at the prince like an idiot. Aditya grins and immediately hides it from his tutor.

In the present, at the edge of the memory, ILAN watches this alone.

> **ILAN**
> *(quietly)*
> I don't remember this.

> **NIZHAL**
> It's not your memory.

---

**EXT. THE TEMPLE STREET, UNDER THE NEEM TREE. EARLIER THAT MORNING.**

While the city waits for the auspicious hour, the boys' games. A clay pot hangs from a rope stretched between two houses, painted with turmeric and strung with flowers: the **uriyadi** pot. Men on the rooftops hold the rope ends and jerk the pot up and down. Women with brass pots of turmeric water wait to drench whoever tries.

YOUNG ADITYA, escaped from his tutor for ten minutes, is blindfolded with a cloth and handed a long stick. He spins around three times. The crowd roars.

> **GAMEPLAY: URIYADI (played as Aditya, about 2 min)**
>
> - The screen goes **almost dark**: you're blindfolded. You hear the crowd, and the clay bell tied inside the pot clinking, louder when you face it (stereo direction on headphones, plus a soft pulse at the screen edge for players without sound).
> - **One voice** guides you through the noise, young Ilan shouting from the side: "Left! Left! *Your* left! Two steps! Up, it's going up!" His calls are always right. The rest of the crowd shouts nonsense on purpose.
> - Swing with **CUT**. The men on the roof jerk the pot higher when you swing too early. Splashes of turmeric water hit the screen (yellow bursts) and briefly drown out the bell.
> - Break the pot: it bursts in a shower of coins, puffed rice and sweets, and every child in the street dives in.

ADITYA pulls off the blindfold, dripping yellow, and the first face he finds in the crowd is young Ilan's, shouting and jumping.

> **ADITYA (V.O.)**
> I couldn't see a thing. I could always hear him.

---

**EXT. THE CAR STREETS. THE AUSPICIOUS HOUR.**

A conch. Then a hundred conches. The nadaswaram players climb onto the chariot's base. The crowd surges to the ropes, men, women, children, guards and potters together, thousands of hands on four ropes.

Along the ropes, troupes of **oyilattam** dancers in matching white with coloured scarves dance in lines, swinging the scarves in unison, setting the rhythm for the pull.

> **GAMEPLAY: VADAM PIDITHAL, PULLING THE CHARIOT (rhythm, about 4 min)**
>
> - Played as young Aditya, who has been allowed down from the platform to touch the rope for the first pull, a royal custom. Ilan pushes in beside him on the rope.
> - **The chant leads.** A lead singer calls and the crowd answers: "*Hara-hara!*" On each answer, **press JUMP to heave**. The chariot creaks forward a few feet with each good heave.
> - **Everyone together:** a heave matters only when it matches the crowd's beat. Off-beat heaves pull the rope sideways and stall the chariot. A row of the rope-pullers nearest the player shows the timing: watch them lean.
> - **The turn:** at the first corner the great wheels lock. Men with long wooden levers (the "brakes", wedges of wood) work at the wheels. Switch to the lever crew and **tap CUT on their call** to lever the wheel around, then back to the rope.
> - **The progress bar is the city:** the more people join the ropes as the chariot moves, the easier each heave. By the last street the crowd is so big the chariot seems to roll on its own.

The chariot reaches its stopping place at the end of the first street. Silence, then a roar. Flowers rain from every rooftop.

---

**EXT. THE CAR STREETS. LATER.**

YOUNG ADITYA and YOUNG ILAN sit on the edge of a temple step, both still yellow from the turmeric water, sharing a leaf of sweet pongal from the temple kitchen. Their hands are raw from the rope.

> **YOUNG ADITYA**
> Who moved it? Me or you?

> **YOUNG ILAN**
> Neither. Everybody.

> **YOUNG ADITYA**
> *(thinking hard)*
> Then nobody gets the credit.

> **YOUNG ILAN**
> *(shrugging, mouth full)*
> It moved, didn't it?

Present-day ILAN, standing in the street behind them, hears himself say it.

> **ILAN**
> I said that.

> **NIZHAL**
> You were eight. You were wiser at eight.

> **ADITYA (V.O.)**
> *(the memory, ending)*
> When I'm king, I'll have him stand next to me. In front, where everyone can see.

> **ADITYA (V.O.)**
> He'll say he doesn't need it. He'll be lying.

The memory blurs into white light and the sound of the chant.

---

**EXT. THE OBSERVATORY. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

ILAN stands up off the star floor. He's very still. Then he looks at the Mirror Temple, its tiers floating in the violet sky.

> **ILAN**
> Nizhal.

> **NIZHAL**
> Hm.

> **ILAN**
> Everyone on the rope. All the people in the shards. I know their names now.

> **NIZHAL**
> So?

> **ILAN**
> So I'm not doing it so they'll remember me.

He starts walking toward the tower. NIZHAL doesn't follow for a long moment. When he finally does, his eyes are red, and they stay red.

## Accuracy notes

- **Ther thiruvizha** (temple car festivals) are central to Tamil temple life: huge carved wooden chariots, ropes called *vadam*, the whole town pulling together, wooden wedge-levers used to steer and brake at corners. The great cars of Thiruvarur and Srivilliputhur are the reference for scale and carving.
- **Uriyadi** is a Tamil festival game: a pot hung on a rope, raised and lowered by people on rooftops, broken blindfolded with a stick while others splash turmeric water.
- **Oyilattam** is a Tamil folk dance in lines with coloured scarves, performed at festivals.
- **Sweet pongal** (sakkarai pongal: rice, moong dal, jaggery, ghee, cardamom) is a temple offering food and period-appropriate. Cardamom is native to the Western Ghats.
