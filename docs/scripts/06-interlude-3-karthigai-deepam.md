# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Interlude III: "Karthigai Deepam" (The Festival of Lamps)

**Placement:** between Chapter 3 and Chapter 4 · **Runtime:** about 12 minutes
**Whose memory:** Ponni the bell-caster's, held in the foundry shard. Through her we meet Kaalavan and his daughter, **Valli**.
**Festival:** Karthigai Deepam, the full-moon night of the month of Karthigai (November–December), when every home lights rows of clay lamps and a great beacon is lit on the hill
**Events played:** lighting the city's lamps across the rooftops before the beacon, drawing a **kolam** at Ponni's threshold, the **sokkapanai** bonfire
**Teaches:** placing light to shape where shadow falls, the exact idea behind Light Bend in Chapter 4
**Arc beat:** the villain as a loving father. The player should like Kaalavan here, and the anklet gets its story.

---

**EXT. THE WAR YARD. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

The bell sound grows. The furnace light goes soft and warm. Night falls.

**EXT. KAMMALAR STREET. KARTHIGAI NIGHT. (TWENTY-ONE YEARS EARLIER)**

The same street from Chapter 3, alive. Night. A full moon is just rising over the roofs, and it is the brightest night of the year, because every surface of every house is lined with **agal vilakku**: small clay lamps, hundreds per house, on steps, sills, walls and roof edges, each a single still flame.

**PONNI (30)**, the bell-caster, broad-armed, her hair tied back and a scar across one forearm, kneels at her threshold with a little pot of rice flour. She lets it run in a thin line from between her thumb and finger, drawing a kolam: dots first, in a grid, then one unbroken line looping around them.

> **PONNI**
> *(to herself, counting)*
> Seven dots, seven rows. One line. Never lift your hand.

> **GAMEPLAY: KOLAM (short, calm, optional to perfect)**
>
> A dot grid is drawn for you. Trace one unbroken line that loops around every dot without crossing itself, by dragging (touch) or with the stick. There are three patterns of rising difficulty. A finished kolam glows faintly and lights the lamps on Ponni's step. If the line goes wrong, Ponni sweeps it away with her palm and you start again: no penalty, it's a kolam.
>
> On mobile this is one of the most relaxing moments in the game; keep it short and tactile.

A man's voice from the street:

> **KAALAVAN (O.S.)**
> Ponni-akka. Have you a lamp left for a small girl?

**KAALAVAN (34)**, younger, no grey yet, a minister's son in plain cotton tonight. On his shoulders sits **VALLI (6)**: round face, a jasmine string slipping out of her hair, and on her ankle a **silver anklet with small bells** that rings every time she kicks.

> **VALLI**
> Appa says I can light one on the roof. The *roof*.

> **PONNI**
> Then you'll need a bronze one. Clay is for the steps.

She takes a small bronze lamp from her shelf, a five-wicked one, and puts it in Valli's hands.

> **KAALAVAN**
> How much?

> **PONNI**
> For her? Nothing. Light it before the hill does, that's my price.

---

**EXT. KANCHI ROOFTOPS. CONTINUOUS.**

KAALAVAN climbs a ladder with Valli; PONNI follows with a basket of clay lamps. From the roof, the whole city is visible: a sea of small lights. Far to the south, on a dark hill, the **beacon** waits unlit: a huge copper cauldron of ghee with a wick as thick as a man.

> **PONNI**
> Every year the street races the hill. Every lamp on our roofs lit before the beacon.

> **GAMEPLAY: RACE THE BEACON (time trial, about 3 min, with Ponni as the player character)**
>
> - Run the rooftops of Kammalar Street lighting lamps: touch an unlit lamp to light it (each lights its neighbours in a short chain, so placement matters).
> - **The twist:** some roof edges are in the shadow of taller roofs, and lamps there **light nothing beyond their own wick** until a lit lamp's glow reaches them. Choose an order so the light spreads into the shade. This is the core idea of Light Bend: light changes where shadow falls.
> - **Lamp-carrying:** carry a lit lamp to a dark section by holding JUMP (you move slower and can't jump while carrying).
> - Valli trails after you on Kaalavan's shoulders and points out lamps you missed ("*There! There!*").
> - Score: lamps lit before the beacon. Light every lamp and the whole street flares at once as the beacon goes up.

On the hill, the beacon lights: a column of flame that turns the clouds orange. From every rooftop in Kanchi the shout goes up together:

> **CROWD**
> *Annamalaikku arogara!*

VALLI, on her father's shoulders, lights her bronze lamp one second after the beacon, and is devastated.

> **VALLI**
> I was late.

> **KAALAVAN**
> You weren't late. The hill was early.

> **VALLI**
> Hills can't be early, Appa.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Then you've never seen a hill.

*(The same rhythm as Sendan's line to Kayal in Interlude II. Fathers in this city make the same joke. Don't point it out.)*

---

**EXT. CROSSROADS. LATER.**

The **sokkapanai**: a tall cone of dry palm leaves bound around a pole, as high as a house, standing at the crossroads in front of the small street temple. The priest touches a torch to its base. It goes up in a roar, sparks pouring into the sky.

Children dance around it. VALLI tugs her father's hand toward the fire, and her anklet rings.

> **VALLI**
> If I'm ever lost, you'll hear me. *(she stamps)* Like that.

> **KAALAVAN**
> I'd hear you from the other side of the world.

Close on the anklet in the firelight.

PONNI, watching from the edge with her arms folded, smiles.

> **PONNI (V.O.)**
> *(the memory's own voice, quiet)*
> That was the last good Karthigai. The rains failed the next year, and the year after.

> **PONNI (V.O.)**
> Half the street went hungry. The minister's girl was one of the ones who didn't wake.

The bonfire collapses inward in a burst of sparks. In the present, standing at the edge of the memory: ILAN, KAYAL, and NIZHAL.

> **KAYAL**
> That anklet.

> **ILAN**
> He wears it on his wrist. He was wearing it this morning.

> **NIZHAL**
> *(almost gently)*
> He wants her back. That's all this is.

> **ILAN**
> *(after a long beat)*
> So would I.

NIZHAL turns his head to look at Ilan for a long moment. His eyes are red, steadily, for the whole shot.

White.

## Accuracy notes

- **Karthigai Deepam** is one of the oldest Tamil festivals, mentioned in Sangam poetry. Homes are lit with rows of clay agal lamps; the great beacon is lit on Thiruvannamalai hill, and the cry is *"Annamalaikku arogara!"* Our hill is fictional and closer to Kanchi; the cry is the same.
- The **sokkapanai** (a tall bonfire of palm leaves lit in front of temples) is a real Karthigai custom.
- **Kolam:** rice-flour designs drawn freehand at thresholds every morning by women; *sikku kolam* is the style drawn as a single unbroken line looping around a dot grid. On festival nights they're larger and more elaborate.
- **Kammalar** is the traditional Tamil name for the artisan communities, including metalworkers.
