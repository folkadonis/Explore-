# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Interlude I: "Eru Thazhuvuthal" (Embracing the Bull)

**Placement:** between Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 · **Runtime:** about 12 minutes · **Playable in the prototype:** yes
**Whose memory:** Ilan's own. The narrator stops the story to show us who he was.
**Festival:** Mattu Pongal, the day of the cattle, second day of the Thai Pongal festival (mid-January)
**Teaches:** timing and rhythm under pressure, which every later Step and Strike relies on
**Arc beat:** the first time Ilan did something great and nobody said his name, except one person

---

**EXT. CORONATION COURTYARD. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

Continuing from the end of Chapter 1. ILAN sits on the mandapa steps below stone ADITYA, elbows on his knees. NIZHAL sits one step lower, mirroring him exactly, which annoys him.

> **NIZHAL**
> You said he never looked down on you. Prove it.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Before I tell you about the stepwell, you should know who I was.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Four Pongals before the coronation. Mattu Pongal. I was sixteen.

The noon light flares white.

---

**EXT. VILLAGE OF SEVVAPPUR, OUTSIDE KANCHI. MORNING. (FOUR YEARS EARLIER)**

The colour floods back: a warm, low Thai-month sun, smoke and birdsong. **This is the only time in the game the world looks fully alive,** so every frame should feel busy.

A village street of red earth, swept and sprinkled with cow-dung water. Rice-flour kolam patterns sit at every threshold, the big Pongal kolams drawn overnight: interlocking loops around dot grids, some filled with red and yellow. Mango-leaf strings (thoranam) hang over doors. Banana plants, whole and fruiting, are tied to both sides of every gate.

Outside each house, a clay pot sits on a three-stone hearth with a turmeric plant tied around its neck, the morning's rice already cooked. Sugarcane stalks lean against the walls in bundles.

At the street's end, the **VAADIVAASAL**: the bull gate. A narrow lane of heavy timber posts opens onto a long stretch of trampled earth fenced with palm-leaf barricades. On both sides, villagers crowd onto platforms of stacked bullock carts. Behind the gate, unseen, bulls stamp and blow.

Two **PARAI DRUMMERS** stand on a cart, sticks a blur. The rhythm is the heartbeat of the whole sequence: fast, hard, and it never stops.

ILAN (16), thinner, hair shorter, a cotton veshti hitched to the knee, stretches his hands at the barricade. Beside him, CAPTAIN VEERAN (41) wears his guard's sash even off duty.

> **VEERAN**
> Your father know you're here?

> **ILAN**
> My father's carving a wall in Kanchi.

> **VEERAN**
> Then I'll be the one who carries you home.

Across the arena, on the elders' platform, sits the young **PRINCE ADITYA (16)**. He is not allowed in the arena. He leans so far over the rail that two attendants hold his sash.

---

**EXT. VAADIVAASAL. CONTINUOUS.**

The **VILLAGE HEADMAN** raises a hand. The drums stop. He calls each bull by name and by house, as is the custom:

> **HEADMAN**
> From the house of Muthan the potter: Vellaiyan!

A white **Kangayam** bull, broad and calm, its horns painted in bands of red and blue with brass caps on the tips. Between the horns is a red cloth tied around a bundle of coins: the **salli kaasu**. Whoever holds the bull may claim it.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> The rule was simple. Hold the hump. Not the horns, not the tail.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Hold on for fifteen strides, and the cloth on his horns is yours.

> **GAMEPLAY: ROUND 1, VELLAIYAN (tutorial bull)**
>
> 1. **The gate.** The bull bursts out and charges down the lane. A ring tightens around his hump. Press **GRIP** (Jump) as it closes. Too early and he hooks you aside; too late and he's past.
> 2. **The hold.** Ilan swings onto the hump and hugs it. The bull bucks. A balance bar shows which way he's throwing you: **lean against it** with the stick or A/D.
> 3. **The beat.** Each time his front hooves hit the ground, the GRIP button pulses. Tap it on the beat to dig your fingers in and refill your grip. Miss the beat and your grip slips.
> 4. **The flag.** Hold on to the marker flag (15 m).
> 5. **The cloth.** At the flag he drops his head to throw you. Tap **SNATCH** (Cut) as his head dips to pull the cloth from his horns.
>
> Scoring: grab timing (Perfect / Good), metres held, cloth taken. Thrown riders tumble into the soft earth and the crowd groans, never anything worse.

Vellaiyan stops, blows, and trots back to his owner as if nothing happened. The crowd laughs and whistles.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Vellaiyan was a gentle bull. Every boy in the district had held him.

---

**EXT. VAADIVAASAL. LATER.**

The sun's higher. Dust hangs gold over the arena.

> **HEADMAN**
> From the house of the Pulikulam herders: Karuppan!

A black **Pulikulam** bull, smaller and fast, with short forward-curved horns and a hump like a fist. He doesn't charge; he turns, looking for someone.

> **VEERAN**
> Not that one. Karuppan spins.

> **ILAN**
> Then I'll spin with him.

> **GAMEPLAY: ROUND 2, KARUPPAN**
>
> Same rules, harder bull: shorter warning before each buck, bucks that switch direction mid-throw, a quicker beat. The hold distance stays at 15 m.

If the player is thrown, VEERAN hauls Ilan back over the barricade by the back of his veshti and dusts him off with two hard slaps.

> **VEERAN**
> Again?

> **ILAN**
> Again.

---

**EXT. VAADIVAASAL. NOON.**

The last bull. The drummers change rhythm: slower, heavier. The crowd goes quiet.

> **HEADMAN**
> From the royal herd, the bull of the palace: Sivappan!

A red-brown bull, enormous, with long lyre-shaped horns painted gold. Nobody has held him in three Pongals. He walks out of the gate slowly, which is worse than running.

On the elders' platform, ADITYA has stopped leaning over the rail. He is gripping it.

> **GAMEPLAY: ROUND 3, SIVAPPAN**
>
> The hardest hold: 20 m. Sivappan pauses before charging, so the ring timing is irregular. He bucks in pairs, and twice he stops dead and spins. The beat shifts as he tires.

---

**EXT. VAADIVAASAL. CONTINUOUS. (AFTER THE HOLD)**

Whatever the player's result, the story runs the same way: Ilan held Sivappan to the flag. The memory insists on it.

ILAN slides off at the far end, staggering, the red cloth from the gold horns balled in his fist. For one heartbeat the arena is completely silent. Then it erupts.

The HEADMAN raises his hand for quiet, and turns, as custom requires, to the owner's house.

> **HEADMAN**
> Sivappan of the palace herd has been held! Honour to the royal house!

The cheer goes up again, for the palace. The elders bow toward the prince's platform. Nobody asks the boy's name. A palace herdsman takes the bull's rope from Ilan's other hand without looking at him.

ILAN stands in the middle of the arena, dust settling on him, the cloth still in his fist.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> That was the rule too. The honour goes to the house that raised the bull.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I told myself I didn't mind.

---

**EXT. BEHIND THE VAADIVAASAL. EVENING.**

Cattle stand in a row being washed and garlanded for the night. The sky is pink. Pongal smoke drifts.

ILAN sits alone on an upturned cart wheel, picking a thorn out of his palm. Footsteps. ADITYA, who has slipped his attendants, sits beside him without asking.

> **ADITYA**
> You held him.

> **ILAN**
> Your house held him. The headman said so.

> **ADITYA**
> I was watching. You held him.

He holds out his hand. In it are three coins from the cloth bundle, palace gold.

> **ADITYA**
> I'm not allowed in the arena. Ever. Did you know that?

> **ILAN**
> No.

> **ADITYA**
> So tell me what it felt like.

Ilan looks at him, then at the coins. He doesn't take them. He starts to tell him instead, hands moving, and the camera pulls back across the evening village, the two boys small on the cart wheel, as the parai starts again for the night's kummi dance.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> He was the only one who said my name that day.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I'd forgotten that. You forget the wrong things.

---

**EXT. CORONATION COURTYARD. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

Back in the frozen courtyard. ILAN on the steps. He looks up at stone Aditya and the gold crown.

> **NIZHAL**
> *(quietly, for once)*
> Fine. He didn't look down on you.

> **ILAN**
> Come on. The stepwell.

He stands. Nizhal stands a half-beat before him.

Chapter card: **II · THE DROWNED STEPWELL**

## Accuracy notes

- **Name:** the Sangam poems (Kalittokai, the *mullaikkali* section) call it *eru thazhuvuthal*, embracing the bull. "Jallikattu" comes from *salli* (coins) and *kattu* (tie): the coin bundle tied to the horns. Use both.
- **Rules shown:** hold the hump only; no grabbing horns or tail; win by holding for a set distance. The bull is never harmed or killed. Honour goes to the owner's house when a bull is held, and to the bull when he isn't. That custom is the emotional hinge of this interlude.
- **Breeds:** Kangayam (white-grey, Kongu country) and Pulikulam (dark, small, fast, from the Madurai and Sivaganga region) are both native Tamil breeds. Sivappan is a royal-herd bull of mixed line.
- **Mattu Pongal details to show:** horns painted and capped with brass, garlands, bells, bathing the cattle, coloured kolams, the turmeric plant tied around the Pongal pot, sugarcane, banana plants and mango leaves at gates, parai drums, kummi dancing at night.
- **Don't show:** bulls being stabbed or hurt, cattle with chillies, modern number vests, plastic, tarmac.
