# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Prologue: "The Last Morning"

**Runtime:** about 10 minutes · **Playable:** about 7 minutes · **Arc stage:** 1, the proud boy
**Teaches:** run, jump, wall-run, ledge grab · **Powers:** none
**Format notes:** Scene headings mark where the camera is. `GAMEPLAY` blocks mark where the player has control. Dialogue lines stay under 15 words (story bible rule). Ilan's voice-over (V.O.) is always past tense.

---

### COLD OPEN

Two versions follow. **B** is recommended (see `docs/story-notes.md`, fix 1B). **A** follows the original bible. Everything after the cold open works with either.

**EXT. MIRROR TEMPLE, OUTER WALL. DUSK. (YEARS LATER)**

Black screen. The sound comes first: *tock... tock...* An iron chisel on granite, unhurried. Somewhere far off, a temple bell. Parrots settling.

FADE IN on an extreme close-up. A chisel edge bites stone and a chip flies past the lens, catching the low sun.

Pull back slowly (50mm, handheld but steady). A bamboo scaffold lashed with coir rope against a black granite wall. On it, **ILAN (32)**: lean, grey coming in at the temples, a carver's forearms crossed with old white scars. On his right upper arm, a pale band of skin where an armband used to sit. He works without looking at his hands.

The frieze beside him runs the length of the scaffold, about thirty figures deep. A palace roof. A boy mid-leap between towers. Elephants standing very still. A shattered disc with rays coming off it.

Every figure has a carved face except one: the running boy. Where his face should be, the stone is smooth.

Below, two **APPRENTICES** (10 and 12) sit on a block of uncut stone, sharing a banana leaf of puffed rice. The younger one points up with a sticky finger.

> **YOUNGER APPRENTICE**
> Anna, who's the one with no face?

> **ILAN**
> *(not stopping)*
> A temple guard. Nobody important.

> **OLDER APPRENTICE**
> Then why's he in every panel?

Ilan's mallet pauses. He blows dust out of a groove.

> **ILAN**
> Because someone should remember what he did.

He sets the chisel to the boy's outstretched hand.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Most people remember Prince Aditya's coronation as an ordinary day.

*Tock.*

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I'm going to tell you what really happened.

MATCH CUT: the carved boy's leap becomes:

**EXT. KANCHI, PALACE ROOFTOPS. MORNING. (THE CORONATION DAY)**

ILAN (20), mid-leap between two tiled roofs, against a pale gold sky. The sun is low and huge in the east, and his shadow races across the tiles below him.

He lands, rolls, keeps running. He wears a veshti tucked high for running, a red waist sash, a bronze armband (no shard yet), and a palm-leaf letter tied with red thread pushed into his sash.

The city spreads below: clay-tile roofs and lime-washed walls, smoke from a thousand cooking fires, rice-flour kolam patterns fresh on every threshold. Above it all, the thirteen tiers of the **MIRROR TEMPLE**, black against the sunrise.

From the palace: the reedy climb of NADASWARAM pipes warming up, the slap of THAVIL drums, a CONCH answering from somewhere across the city.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I was the fastest runner in the temple guard. Ask anyone.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> That morning I carried a letter from the Mirror-keeper to the palace.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I never asked what it said. I was late.

> **GAMEPLAY: ROOFTOP RUN (tutorial, about 4 min, no fail state; falls respawn at the last ledge)**
>
> 1. **Run** along a roof ridge. Pigeons lift off ahead of him; they mark the route.
> 2. **Jump** a 3 m alley gap. Below, a woman sweeping a kolam looks up and shouts. (Loading tip: *"Guards don't jump between roofs. I did."*)
> 3. **Wall-run** along the painted gallery of the royal storehouse: ochre and indigo fresco panels of a river crossing.
> 4. **Ledge grab** onto a gopuram tier. Close-up of his hand finding a groove worn by other hands.
>    ILAN (V.O.): *"My father carved these walls. I knew every handhold."*
> 5. **Slide** down a flag rope into the back of the coronation courtyard. The rope burns his palms; he shakes them out, grinning.

**EXT. CORONATION COURTYARD. CONTINUOUS.**

Crane up and over the crowd: three thousand people in a granite courtyard. Silk canopies, rolled tight on their poles, wait for the noon heat. Strings of jasmine and orange kanakambaram flowers loop between the pillars. Smoke rises blue-grey from ghee lamps.

On a raised mandapa: **PRINCE ADITYA (20)**, bare-chested, sandal paste on his brow, sits very straight. To his left, the **ODHUVARS** sing a Tevaram hymn in unison. Priests stand ready with golden pots of river water for the anointing. Before Aditya, on a bronze tray, the **CROWN**: a tall gold mukuta, plain except for one carved tiger.

Four temple elephants stand in a row at the foot of the steps, their foreheads painted, bells at their necks.

ILAN pushes along the back wall to the guards' line, breathing hard. **CAPTAIN VEERAN (45)**, the guard captain, broad and patient, puts a spear shaft across his chest without looking at him.

> **VEERAN**
> Back row, carver's son.

> **ILAN**
> I grew up with him.

> **VEERAN**
> So did half the city. Back row.

ILAN looks over Veeran's shoulder. On the mandapa, ADITYA's eyes move across the crowd and pass over Ilan without stopping. Ilan half-raises a hand, then turns it into scratching his neck.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> He didn't see me. I told myself he couldn't.

---

**INT. TEMPLE CLOISTER (TIRUCHUTRU MALIGAI). CONTINUOUS.**

The long pillared walkway around the courtyard. Here it's suddenly quiet and cool, and the hymn is muffled. Light falls through the pillars in bars.

ILAN cuts through, heading for the palace door. A voice stops him.

> **KAALAVAN (O.S.)**
> Sengan's son. The stone-carver.

**KAALAVAN (55)** steps out from between two pillars: silk angavastram, grey beard trimmed close, gold rings on three fingers. A bronze rod of office, waist-high, is tucked under one arm.

Insert: on Kaalavan's left wrist, half-hidden by his sleeve, is a **child's silver anklet** (kolusu), tied on with thread. It doesn't fit an adult. He wears it anyway.

> **ILAN**
> Minister. I have a letter for the prince.

> **KAALAVAN**
> I'll see he gets it.

Kaalavan holds out his hand. A beat. Ilan gives it to him. Kaalavan glances at the red thread, recognises the keeper's knot, and tucks the letter into his sleeve without opening it.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Your father built the Mirror Temple's outer wall. Did you know?

> **ILAN**
> Everyone knows. Nobody remembers his name.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Mayan's door remembers. It opens only for the blood of its builders.

He steps closer and lowers his voice, as though they're sharing a joke.

> **KAALAVAN**
> The new king must look into the Mirror today. The keepers are slow.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Bring it down before noon. Only you can.

> **ILAN**
> Why not ask the keepers?

> **KAALAVAN**
> Because they'd ask the prince. And you'd be in the back row.

He lets that land.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Do this, and your name goes in stone. Beside the kings.

Ilan looks past him, through the pillars, at the Mirror Temple filling the sky.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I should have asked why the keeper's letter mattered so much.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I didn't ask anything. I ran.

He's already gone. Kaalavan watches him go and, without looking down, touches the anklet at his wrist.

SMASH CUT TO BLACK.

Title card, carved letters filling with violet light:

**SHADOWS OF KANCHI**

## Death and restart lines (stage 1, cocky)

Ilan corrects his own story. Play one at random, never the same one twice in a row.

1. "Wait. I'm telling it wrong."
2. "I'd never fall for that. Again."
3. "Ask anyone. I didn't fall."

## Environmental puzzle ideas (no powers yet)

1. **Garland run.** A flower garland strung between two towers sags under Ilan's weight. Keep running and it holds; stop on it and it tears and drops you one level. The flowers burst into a cloud of orange petals that hang in the air for the rest of the run.
2. **Pigeon tell.** Pigeons only sit on sound roof tiles. Loose tiles are empty. The player learns to read the roof by watching the birds, a skill that pays off in Ch. 1 when the birds are stone.

## Lore inscriptions (carved tablets on the rooftops; Kayal reads these aloud in later chapters)

1. *"Mayan raised this tower in one lifetime. He did not live to see it shine."*
2. *"The king looks once. The Mirror looks back forever."*
3. *"Sengan, carver, set these stones."* (cut later, in a smaller and less practised hand: a fellow mason's tribute)
4. *"Whatever is lost lives in its shadow. Whatever is shadow, the Mirror keeps."*
5. *"Do not wake the Mirror for want. It answers want with cost."*

## Production notes

- **Who wrote the letter:** Keeper SENDAN, Kayal's father. It reads: *"Do not let the Mirror leave the tower. The minister has asked for the door."* Ilan finds it half-burnt at the end of Ch. 1, and Kayal recognises her father's hand in Ch. 2. This is the first concrete proof that Ilan was used, and it gives his guilt a physical object.
- **Kaalavan's anklet** is Valli's. Show it without comment in the prologue, Ch. 1 and Ch. 4. In Ch. 5, when Valli's shadow won't come to him, he unties it and lets it fall into the Mirror.
- **Period accuracy:** no marigolds (they arrived in India in the 1500s) and no chillies, tomatoes or potatoes in any food. Use jasmine, kanakambaram, lotus, oleander and screwpine; rice, ghee, jaggery, black pepper, plantain and coconut.
