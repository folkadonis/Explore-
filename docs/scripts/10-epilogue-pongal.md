# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Epilogue: "Pongalo Pongal"

**Runtime:** about 15 minutes (playable) · **Arc stage:** after; Ilan is whole and unseen
**Festival:** Thai Pongal, the harvest festival, one year after the coronation: Bhogi, Thai Pongal, Mattu Pongal and Kaanum Pongal over four days
**Events played:** the **Bhogi** fire, the **Pongal pot** boiling over, the Mattu Pongal **jallikattu**, now from the barricade
**Bookends:** the prologue's coronation morning, Interlude I's jallikattu, and the carved wall

---

**EXT. CORONATION COURTYARD. MORNING. (THE CORONATION DAY, REWOUND)**

The prologue's opening, shot for shot, but now we know everything. The nadaswaram. The crowd. The rolled silk canopies. The jasmine.

ADITYA sits on the mandapa. The priests anoint him. He lifts the crown and sets it level on his own head, and the courtyard roars.

He scans the crowd, as he always does. His eyes pass over the back row, and stop on a guard there. A young man in a red sash, bareheaded, clapping with everyone else.

ADITYA frowns, as if trying to remember a word. Then he looks away.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Nobody remembered the curse. Nobody remembered me.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> The coronation was an ordinary day. That's what I paid for.

In a corner of the courtyard, **KAALAVAN**, older and quieter, stands with his hand over his empty wrist. He can't remember why it feels empty. He walks out of the court and doesn't come back.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Kaalavan left court that evening. He'd dreamed of a small girl with bells on her feet.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I heard he went to the drought country in the south and built granaries there.

On the steps of the Mirror Temple, **KEEPER SENDAN** is alive and arguing with a younger keeper about lamp oil. His daughter is not beside him. She's halfway up the tower already, with the ledger.

---

**EXT. KANCHI. BHOGI, THE LAST DAY OF MARGAZHI. DAWN. (ONE YEAR LATER)**

Dawn. In every street, a small **Bhogi** fire of old mats, worn-out baskets and broken things, burned to clear the house for the new year. Children beat small drums around the fires (the *bhogi kottu*).

ILAN, a year older, carries a broken cart wheel to the fire on his street and drops it in. He has no shadow. Nobody notices: shadows are the last thing anybody looks at.

> **GAMEPLAY: BHOGI (short, free movement)**
>
> Walk your street at dawn. Carry old things to the fire. People greet you as a neighbour; nobody knows what you did. Stand by the fire as long as you like.

---

**EXT. THE STREET OUTSIDE ILAN'S HOUSE. THAI PONGAL. SUNRISE.**

The first day of Thai. A fresh kolam at every door. Sugarcane tied in an arch over every threshold. Outside each house: a new clay pot on a three-stone hearth, a turmeric plant tied around its neck, milk and new-harvest rice inside it, and the whole street waiting for one thing.

ILAN kneels at his own pot. Next door, an old woman, a small boy and a baby are doing the same.

> **GAMEPLAY: THE PONGAL POT (short and tactile)**
>
> Feed the fire with palm fronds to bring the pot to the boil: tap to add fuel; too much and the flame roars and you have to wait; too little and it dies. The pot must **boil over** (*pongu*) toward the east, toward the rising sun, the good omen. When the milk foams over the rim, everyone on the street shouts it together:
>
> **"Pongalo Pongal!"**
>
> Stir in jaggery, ghee and cardamom (no cashews: they arrived with the Portuguese), then offer the first portion to the sun on a plantain leaf.

The small boy next door shouts it loudest. He's about eight, skinny, missing a front tooth: **THE APPRENTICE**, from the cold open, younger. His name is **MURUGAN**.

> **MURUGAN**
> Anna! Yours boiled over to the east! That's lucky.

> **ILAN**
> Yours did too.

> **MURUGAN**
> Mine went west a bit.

> **ILAN**
> West is lucky too. Ask anyone.

---

**EXT. SEVVAPPUR VILLAGE. MATTU PONGAL. MORNING.**

Interlude I's village, one year after the coronation. The same vaadivaasal. The same parai drums. The cattle washed and painted, horns banded in red and blue, garlanded.

MURUGAN, eight and determined, is trying to climb the barricade into the arena. He's far too small. ILAN catches him by the back of his veshti exactly the way Captain Veeran once caught Ilan, and sets him down.

> **MURUGAN**
> I'm going to hold Vellaiyan's grandson. Everyone says he's gentle.

> **ILAN**
> Everyone said that about his grandfather. Feet first. Watch his front hooves.

> **GAMEPLAY: FROM THE BARRICADE (short)**
>
> You don't enter the arena. You coach. As the gentle young bull comes out of the gate, call the beat for Murugan: **tap GRIP on his hoof-falls** to shout "Now! Now!", and Murugan's hold improves with every call on the beat. It's the jallikattu rhythm from Interlude I, used for someone else.

MURUGAN holds the young bull to the flag. Then, as always, the headman turns to the owner's house:

> **HEADMAN**
> Held! Honour to the house of Muthan the potter!

The crowd cheers for the potter's house. Murugan stands in the arena with the red cloth, alone, the way Ilan once stood.

ILAN climbs onto the barricade, cups his hands, and shouts loud enough for the whole village to hear:

> **ILAN**
> **Murugan! Murugan held him!**

Heads turn. A few people laugh and take it up: "Murugan!" Murugan looks at the barricade, finds Ilan's face, and grins with his missing tooth.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I learned that from a prince. Somebody has to say the name.

---

**EXT. MIRROR TEMPLE, OUTER WALL. DUSK. (YEARS LATER)**

The cold open from the prologue. Older ILAN (32) on the bamboo scaffold, chiselling. The two apprentices on the stone block below, one of them MURUGAN, older now, eating puffed rice.

The frieze is finished: the running boy, the elephants, the shattered disc, a bull with a boy on its hump, a great chariot pulled by a hundred tiny figures, a girl with a bow. And the running boy, in every panel, with no face.

> **MURUGAN**
> Anna, finish his face. It's been years.

> **ILAN**
> It is finished.

He climbs down. A woman is waiting at the foot of the scaffold, in the indigo and gold of the Mirror-keeper, a palm-leaf ledger under her arm: **KAYAL (31)**. Grey threads in her braid now. She looks at the frieze for a long time.

> **KAYAL**
> It's a good story. Hard to believe.

> **ILAN**
> Most good stories are.

He smiles at her, nods politely as to a stranger, and walks away along the wall into the low sun.

The camera stays on the ground. The scaffold casts a long shadow. The children cast long shadows. Kayal casts a long shadow.

**Ilan casts none.**

KAYAL looks down at the bare ground where his shadow should be. She opens the ledger, without needing to search, to one leaf tied with red thread, and reads her own handwriting:

*"He casts no shadow now. If you are reading this, you knew him. Believe it."*

She closes the ledger. She smiles. She remembers nothing, and she believes it.

> **KAYAL**
> *(to the empty wall, quietly)*
> Ilanthiraiyan.

CUT TO BLACK.

---

**POST-CREDITS**

The Vegavathi at night. The river is low. In the silt at the bottom, a sliver of black glass glints violet. Beside it, the ripples of the water cast moving shadows on the riverbed, and one of them stops moving when the others do.

## Accuracy notes

- **Thai Pongal** runs over four days: **Bhogi** (clearing the old, burning old things), **Thai Pongal** (the pot boiled over toward the sun, *"Pongalo Pongal!"*), **Mattu Pongal** (honouring the cattle; jallikattu), **Kaanum Pongal** (visiting family, outings). The boil-over is the central omen.
- **Pot details:** a new clay pot; a turmeric plant tied around its neck; sugarcane at the door; milk and new rice; jaggery, ghee and cardamom. **No cashews** (introduced by the Portuguese in the 16th century).
- **Bhogi kottu** (small drums children beat around the Bhogi fire) is a real custom.
- The epilogue moves the story's river from the bible's Kaveri to the **Vegavathi**, which actually flows through Kanchipuram.
