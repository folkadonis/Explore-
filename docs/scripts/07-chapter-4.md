# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Chapter 4: "The Thousand-Pillar Hall"

**Runtime:** about 45 minutes · **Region:** the royal library and observatory · **Arc stage:** 4, the tempted
**New power:** Light Bend (turn polished bronze mirrors and sundial gnomons to throw light, and with it, new shadows)
**Set piece:** Nizhal's offer, Kayal's capture, and a survival duel with Kaalavan's projection

---

**EXT. THE THOUSAND-PILLAR HALL. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

A vast pillared hall on a raised plinth, its roof flat and its pillars in long colonnades, so many they blur into a forest of stone. Each pillar is carved differently: a dancer, a horse rearing with a warrior, a monkey eating fruit, a scene of two men playing pallanguzhi. At its far end stands the **observatory**: an open stone platform with a great sundial and a raised star-map floor.

But inside the hall, it's night. The shard in the observatory has made its own sky: cool moonlight falls through the roof's square openings.

> **KAYAL**
> The library's at this end. The observatory's at the other. The shard's there.

> **ILAN**
> And between?

> **KAYAL**
> A thousand pillars. And the palm-leaf library of Vel Nadu.

---

**INT. THE PALM-LEAF LIBRARY. CONTINUOUS.**

Wooden racks to the ceiling, holding bundles of palm-leaf manuscripts: each bundle a stack of dried leaves strung between two wooden boards, tied with cord. Thousands of them. The smell of old leaf and the oil used to preserve it.

Kayal moves through it like a person in her own house.

> **KAYAL**
> Medicine. Grammar. Temple accounts. Poetry. Every copy a hand made.

> **KAYAL**
> When a leaf crumbles, the scribes copy it onto a new one. Forever.

She pulls one bundle from a rack without even looking, opens it to a leaf, and hands it to Ilan.

> **KAYAL**
> Read.

Insert: Tamil script scratched into the leaf. ILAN reads it aloud slowly, in Tamil, then the subtitle:

> **ILAN**
> *"Ozhukkam vizhuppam tharalan, ozhukkam uyirinum ombap padum."*

> *Subtitle: "Right conduct gives greatness. Guard it more than life itself." (Tirukkural, 131)*

> **ILAN**
> Why this one?

> **KAYAL**
> Because it doesn't say "and everyone will know."

> **GAMEPLAY: THE LIBRARY (stealth and traversal)**
>
> - **Lamp-bearers** appear: Nizhalars carrying bronze lamps with violet-white shard-flames that erase shade in a 4 m radius. They patrol the aisles. Kill them first, from behind, with a Strike.
> - Climb the manuscript racks, which throw tall narrow shadows; Step along them in the dark.
> - Kayal reads the rack labels and calls out the safe path: "Medicine, then grammar, then astronomy. The astronomy room has the mirrors."

---

**INT. THE MIRROR ROOM. CONTINUOUS.**

A round chamber ringed with polished bronze mirrors on swivelling stands, each the size of a shield, used by the astronomers to throw sunlight into the hall's dark corners for reading.

> **KAYAL**
> The astronomers turned these to read at night by moonlight.

> **ILAN**
> And to make shadows.

> **GAMEPLAY: LIGHT BEND (teach)**
>
> - Stand by a mirror and hold **CUT** to turn it. A beam of moonlight swings across the room. Anything the beam hits casts a new shadow on the far side.
> - Aim a beam at a pillar so its new shadow falls across a gap. Step into it.
> - Beams also burn Lamp-bearers' shadow-flames out: aim the light at the lamp and the bearer's shade-erasing radius collapses.
> - Mirrors can bounce into other mirrors. Two bounces is the most the chapter ever asks for.

---

**INT. THE THOUSAND PILLARS. LATER.**

> **GAMEPLAY BLOCKS (about 15 min)**
>
> 1. **The pillar maze.** Moonlight through the roof-holes makes pools of light between the pillars; shade runs in long lanes behind each column. Turn mirrors to close light pools and open shadow lanes. Lamp-bearers patrol.
> 2. **The reading gallery.** Upper walkways with gaps. Anchor on a mirror's stand to hold its angle while you cross on the shadow it throws.
> 3. **The shadow clock (nizhal kadigai).** A courtyard sundial: a tall gnomon over a floor marked in *nazhigai* (the old Tamil hour, 24 minutes). The door opens at the *third nazhigai after noon*. Turn the mirrors so moonlight throws the gnomon's shadow onto that mark.
>    **KAYAL:** "My father taught me to tell time by my own shadow. Feet of shadow, aal nizhal."

---

**EXT. THE OBSERVATORY. CONTINUOUS.**

The open top of the hall. A huge circular floor of black stone inlaid with copper stars: the 27 nakshatras, each a cluster of copper studs with its name carved beside it in Tamil. It can turn. At its centre, on a bronze stand, the last shard floats in a column of moonlight.

ILAN reaches for it.

> **NIZHAL**
> Wait.

Ilan stops. NIZHAL steps out in front of him and, for the first time, stands **between** him and something he wants.

> **NIZHAL**
> You heard Mayan. You heard the carving. Mend it, and you vanish.

> **NIZHAL**
> Nobody will ever know your name. Not Aditya. Not her.

> **NIZHAL**
> Leave it broken. Kanchi stays like this, and you're the only one awake in it.

> **NIZHAL**
> You'd be king of the whole city. Every name in it would be yours to remember.

> **NIZHAL**
> And I'd be free. Is that so bad?

ILAN doesn't answer. KAYAL, behind him, stops breathing.

> **KAYAL**
> Ilan.

He still doesn't answer. It's a long beat, too long, held on his face. Something in it is considering.

NIZHAL smiles and drops into the floor like water into sand.

Around the observatory's rim, all at once, shadows stand up: dozens of Nizhalars, Lamp-bearers among them, and at their head, walking out of the moonlight, **KAALAVAN**. Shards orbit him slowly, each with a shadow moving inside it. The anklet is on his wrist.

> **KAALAVAN**
> Thank you for opening the door, boy. And thank you for that silence.

> **KAYAL**
> *(bow up)*
> Stay back.

The Nizhalars take her from behind in one fluid movement. Her bow clatters on the copper stars.

> **ILAN**
> Kayal!

> **KAALAVAN**
> She's not hurt. She'll come to the tower with me. You'll follow.

> **KAALAVAN**
> You and I are the same, you know. We just want what we're owed.

> **GAMEPLAY: THE STAR-MAP FLOOR (puzzle under pressure, about 4 min)**
>
> The shard's stand is sealed until the nakshatras align. Turn the floor's three rings to set **Karthigai** (the Pleiades), **Thiruvathirai** (Ardra) and **Rohini** in a line with the moon's beam. Each correct alignment throws a long shadow of the copper star-cluster across the platform, a new Step path. Lamp-bearers attack while you work; Kayal's voice calls the star names from where she's held ("Thiruvathirai is the red one, the dancer's star!").

---

**EXT. THE OBSERVATORY. THE DUEL.**

The floor locks. The shard rises from its stand. KAALAVAN raises a hand and a projection of himself, made of shards and violet light, steps forward to guard it. The real Kaalavan backs away into the dark with Kayal.

> **BOSS: KAALAVAN'S PROJECTION (survival fight, about 90 seconds)**
>
> - **Goal on screen:** *"Hold the shard pedestal until the pillars align."* A ring of 12 pillars around the platform rises and sinks; when all 12 are up (90 s), the pedestal unlocks.
> - The projection fires shard-light that wipes out shadow in a line. Bend mirrors to keep at least one pillar's shadow near the pedestal at all times.
> - Strike the projection from shadow to stagger it and buy time. It cannot be killed.
> - **Success is surviving.** The scene below plays either way.

The pillars lock. The pedestal opens. KAALAVAN, from the dark, closes his fist, and the shard flies to him instead. The projection dissolves. He's gone, with Kayal and the shard.

---

**EXT. THE OBSERVATORY. LATER.**

The false moon fades. Ordinary noon sun pours back in, harsh and white, onto the star floor.

ILAN sits alone on the copper stars. Kayal's bow lies beside him. NIZHAL is in the floor somewhere; he doesn't come out.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> He said we were the same. He was right. That was the worst of it.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I'd spent my whole life wanting what I was owed.

He picks up the bow. He looks at the Tirukkural leaf, still pushed into his sash.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Nobody owed me anything.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> Kayal didn't owe me the truth. Aditya didn't owe me his coins. My father didn't owe the city a statue.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> They gave it anyway.

He stands. Across the city, the Mirror Temple has changed: its thirteen tiers have come apart and float in pieces in a violet sky.

NIZHAL rises out of the floor beside him, slowly. They don't look at each other.

> **NIZHAL**
> You're going to the tower.

> **ILAN**
> I'm going for her.

The shard-glow in Ilan's armband pulses, and with it, far off, comes the sound of a great crowd chanting in rhythm, and of wood creaking.

Chapter card: **INTERLUDE IV · THER THIRUVIZHA**

## Death and restart lines (stage 4, tempted)

1. "...Let me think about how that part went."
2. "I could have stopped there. I didn't."

## Lore inscriptions

1. *"Twenty-seven stars, twenty-seven houses of the moon. Learn them by name."* (observatory rim)
2. *"A man's shadow at noon: one foot. At the third nazhigai: three."* (shadow clock)
3. *"The copyist's hand forgets, the leaf remembers."* (library)
4. *"Thiruvathirai, the red star. On its night, the Lord dances."* (star floor; Arudra Darshan)
5. *"Ozhukkam vizhuppam tharalan."* (a pillar; Tirukkural 131)

## Accuracy notes

- **Tirukkural** (around the 5th century CE) predates our setting and was widely known in Chola times; kural 131 is quoted correctly.
- **Nazhigai** (24 minutes) is the traditional Tamil unit of time. Telling time by the length of one's own shadow (*aal nizhal*, man-shadow in feet) is a real folk method.
- The **27 nakshatras** carry Tamil names (Karthigai, Rohini, Thiruvathirai, Poosam, Magam, Chithirai, Thiruvonam and so on). Thiruvathirai is associated with Nataraja's dance (Arudra Darshan).
- **Thousand-pillar halls** (aayiram kaal mandapam) are a real South Indian temple form; most surviving examples are later than our period, so ours is the earliest of its kind in-world.
