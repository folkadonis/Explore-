# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Interlude II: "Aadi Perukku" (The Rising of the River)

**Placement:** between Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 · **Runtime:** about 15 minutes
**Whose memory:** Kayal's, held in the stepwell shard. She was twelve.
**Festival:** Aadi Perukku, the 18th day of the month of Aadi (early August), when the monsoon swells the rivers and families give thanks on the banks
**Events played:** a **silambam** bout (staff fighting), a **kabaddi** raid, and the **floating of lamps** on the river
**Teaches:** strike timing and reading an opponent's rhythm, the foundation for Shadow Strike in Chapter 3
**Arc beat:** Kayal and Ilan met years ago and both forgot. Kayal's father is alive and kind on screen.

---

**EXT. THE KALYANI, DROWNED COURT. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

The shard pulls them both in. Light, then sound: water, a lot of it, moving.

---

**EXT. BANKS OF THE VEGAVATHI RIVER, KANCHI. MORNING. (SEVEN YEARS EARLIER)**

The river is in spate: brown, fast and full to its banks after the monsoon. Steps lead down to it, crowded with families. It's a bright day with heavy clouds moving fast.

**Aadi Perukku**, at the water's edge:

- Women in new saris lay offerings on plantain leaves: lamps, flowers, turmeric, and seven kinds of rice (lemon, tamarind, coconut, curd, jaggery) in small heaps. Married women tie new yellow threads at their throats; girls float lamps.
- A newly married couple lets the wife's old wedding garland float away on the river, as custom requires.
- Children run everywhere with sugarcane, mouths full.

KAYAL (12), hair in two braids with jasmine, is crouched at the water with her father, **SENDAN (48)**, the Mirror-keeper: a slight man, grey-bearded, his fingers stained with the same ink as hers.

He is teaching her to set a lamp on a plantain-leaf boat so it won't tip.

> **SENDAN**
> Low and flat. A lamp falls over because it's proud.

> **KAYAL**
> Lamps can't be proud, Appa.

> **SENDAN**
> Then you've never seen a lamp.

In the present, watching: ILAN and present-day KAYAL stand at the edge of the memory, unseen by everyone. Kayal can't take her eyes off her father.

> **ILAN**
> That's him?

> **KAYAL**
> *(barely)*
> That's him.

---

**EXT. RIVERBANK, THE GUARDS' GROUND. LATER.**

A flat sandy space above the bank where the temple guard puts on a display every Aadi Perukku. A ring of spectators. Drums. A pole stacked with bamboo staffs, each one cut to the height of its owner's brow.

CAPTAIN VEERAN (38) is in the ring with a staff, beating every young guard who steps in. He's not cruel about it; he's just better.

ILAN (13), not yet a guard, pushes through the crowd and takes a staff that's too long for him.

> **VEERAN**
> That's a guard's staff.

> **ILAN**
> I'll be a guard.

> **VEERAN**
> Then show me your feet.

---

> **GAMEPLAY: SILAMBAM (rhythm duel against Veeran, 3 rounds)**
>
> Silambam is a footwork art first. The staff follows the feet. The duel is built on that:
>
> - **Feet:** a footwork pattern (kaaladi) is drawn in the sand as a ring of marks. Veeran steps from mark to mark on a drum beat. Move to the mark opposite him on the beat; off the beat, you're open.
> - **Guard:** when Veeran strikes, a flash shows the line (high, low, sweep). Press **JUMP** to guard high, **STEP** to guard low, **CUT** to counter. A guard exactly on the beat makes a clean *tock* of bamboo and opens a counter window.
> - **Counter:** after a perfect guard, CUT strikes back. Three clean counters win a round.
> - **The spin:** in round 3 Veeran uses the full spinning guard (the staff becomes a blur and every strike bounces off). The only way through is to wait for the beat where he changes hands, which the drum marks with a single off-beat.
>
> Losing is expected. Round 3 is written so that a 13-year-old can't win it: if the player does win, Veeran laughs out loud and gives him the staff. Either way, the scene below plays.

ILAN ends flat on his back in the sand, the staff tip resting lightly on his chest.

> **VEERAN**
> Good feet. Bad temper. Come back when you've lost the temper.

The crowd laughs. Ilan gets up red-faced and pushes out of the ring, and nearly knocks over a girl with a plantain-leaf lamp boat: KAYAL, twelve. The lamp survives. She glares at him.

> **YOUNG KAYAL**
> You lost.

> **YOUNG ILAN**
> I didn't lose. He's the captain.

> **YOUNG KAYAL**
> Then you lost to the captain.

He stalks away. Present-day KAYAL watches her younger self with her mouth open.

> **KAYAL**
> That was you?

> **ILAN**
> That was you?

> **NIZHAL**
> *(delighted)*
> You two have *history*.

**EXT. RIVERBANK, THE BOYS' GROUND. AFTERNOON.**

Under the banyan trees, a game of **kabaddi**: two teams of boys on a court marked in the dirt with a stick, a line down the middle. The raider crosses the line chanting "*kabaddi-kabaddi-kabaddi*" on a single held breath, tags whoever he can, and must get back over the line before he runs out of breath or is pinned.

YOUNG ILAN, still angry, is on one team. He raids.

> **GAMEPLAY: KABADDI (one raid, then two more if the player wants a higher score)**
>
> - **Breath:** hold **JUMP** to chant. A breath meter drains while you're over the line. Release it, or let it run out, and you're out.
> - **Tag:** touch defenders by running into them. Each tag scores.
> - **Escape:** defenders link arms and close in from the sides. Dodge with quick direction changes and get back over the centre line still chanting.
> - **The ankle hold:** a defender dives for your ankle. Tap **STEP** at the moment of contact to kick free (one free escape per raid).
>
> Greed is the trap: every extra tag means less breath to get home with.

Whatever happens, in the story young Ilan tags three, is too proud to turn back for home, and runs out of breath one foot short of the line. The opposing team pins him. Under the banyan, a small girl with two braids claps slowly and on purpose.

> **ILAN (V.O.)**
> I always went for one more. Even then.

---

**EXT. RIVERBANK, STEPS. DUSK.**

Hundreds of small flames move on the dark water: the lamp boats of Aadi Perukku, sailing downstream in a slow, wide river of light.

SENDAN and YOUNG KAYAL kneel on the bottom step with the last lamp. Present-day KAYAL kneels just behind them, in the memory, close enough to touch his shoulder.

> **SENDAN**
> This one is for your mother. Set it so it reaches the bend.

> **GAMEPLAY: FLOATING THE LAMP (quiet, no fail state)**
>
> Guide one lamp boat downstream to the bend, through other lamps, floating garlands and eddies. Lean it with the stick; tap JUMP to give it a little push off the shallows. If it tips, Sendan sets another one down without a word and you start again. When it reaches the bend, it joins a hundred other lights and the camera lifts away.

SENDAN puts his arm around his daughter. Present-day KAYAL, unseen, rests her forehead against his back. He doesn't feel it. She knows he can't.

> **KAYAL**
> *(to the memory)*
> I'll finish it, Appa. I'll finish your work.

> **SENDAN**
> *(to young Kayal, not hearing)*
> You already know more than I did at your age. Don't let anyone tell you otherwise.

The lamps blur into light. White.

---

**EXT. THE KALYANI, DROWNED COURT. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

Back. KAYAL is kneeling exactly as she was in the memory. She wipes her face fast with the heel of her hand and stands.

> **ILAN**
> Kayal.

> **KAYAL**
> Don't.

She picks up her bow.

> **KAYAL**
> The next shard is in the foundry. Bronze Guardians. You'll have to fight.

> **ILAN**
> I can fight.

> **KAYAL**
> *(a ghost of a smile)*
> You lost to the captain.

## Accuracy notes

- **Aadi Perukku** (Aadi 18) is the Tamil festival for the rivers rising with the monsoon; offerings of mixed rices, yellow threads, floating lamps, and newly-weds releasing their wedding garlands are all real customs. It's most famous on the Kaveri, but it's celebrated on every river; **Kanchi's river is the Vegavathi** (with the Palar nearby), so that's the river used here.
- **Silambam** is the ancient Tamil staff art; the staff is traditionally bamboo, cut to the user's height. Footwork patterns (kaaladi) drawn on the ground are part of real training.
- **Kabaddi**, also called *sadugudu* in Tamil Nadu, is played as shown: one raider, one held breath, chanting, tagging and returning.
- **Chola-period food check:** rice dishes flavoured with tamarind, lemon, coconut, curd, sesame and jaggery are all fine. No chilli: use black pepper, ginger and mustard seed.
