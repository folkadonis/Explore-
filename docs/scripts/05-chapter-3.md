# SHADOWS OF KANCHI
## Chapter 3: "The Bronze Foundry"

**Runtime:** about 45 minutes · **Region:** the casters' quarter (Kammalar street) · **Arc stage:** 3, the partner
**New power:** Shadow Strike (attack from inside a shadow; the only thing that breaks bronze)
**Boss:** the Bronze Colossus, a 30-foot dwarapala (temple door-guardian) cast for the coronation and never installed

---

**EXT. KAMMALAR STREET, THE CASTERS' QUARTER. NOON. (THE STOPPED HOUR)**

A long street of workshops with low tiled roofs, every doorway black with soot. The air hangs thick with smoke that stopped mid-drift. Stacked outside each shop: clay moulds the size of men, coils of beeswax going soft in the sun, sacks of river clay, baskets of charcoal.

Through every open door, bronze glows: half-finished deities, lamps, bells, all lit orange from within by furnaces that froze at full heat.

> **KAYAL**
> The Kammalar cast every bronze in Vel Nadu. Every lamp, every bell.

> **KAYAL**
> The Nataraja in the palace temple came from this street.

> **ILAN**
> The furnaces are still burning.

> **KAYAL**
> Frozen burning. Don't touch anything orange.

> **Rule for this chapter:** furnace light is orange, and orange light **erases shadow** the same way sunlight does. Each furnace pulses on a slow breath, about every four seconds: bright (no shade near it), then dim (shade returns). The whole chapter is played on that rhythm.

---

**EXT. FOUNDRY GATE. CONTINUOUS.**

The foundry yard is guarded by two life-size bronze **dwarapalas**, temple door-guardians with clubs. Their eyes glow faintly violet. They turn their heads toward Ilan.

> **NIZHAL**
> The shard's woken them. They want it back.

ILAN swings his blade at the nearest. It rings off the bronze like a temple bell and numbs his arm to the shoulder.

> **KAYAL**
> You can't cut bronze, you fool.

> **NIZHAL**
> He can't. I can.

> **GAMEPLAY: SHADOW STRIKE (teach)**
>
> - Stand in shade and press **CUT** near an enemy: Ilan sinks into the shadow at his feet and **strikes from inside it**, a violet slash that comes up out of the ground.
> - **Bronze Guardians** can only be hurt by a Strike from shadow. Attacks from sunlight or furnace light bounce off.
> - Strikes chain: a perfect hit, timed on the guardian's swing as in the silambam duel, gives a short window for a second Strike.
> - Nizhal is visibly part of every Strike now. His arm comes up out of the ground with Ilan's.

---

**GAMEPLAY BLOCKS: THE FOUNDRY (about 25 min)**

> 1. **The wax room.** Rows of wax models (deities, lamps, a dancing girl) sit in a cool, dark room, and every one of them throws a shadow. Guardians patrol. A stealth room: Step from shadow to shadow, Strike from behind.
> 2. **The clay pit.** Workers' baskets hang from a pulley crane. Anchor on the crane's counterweight to hold a basket high; its shadow lets you cross a pit of soft clay.
> 3. **The pouring floor.** Channels of molten bronze run from the main crucible to the moulds. Anchor on a sluice lever to divert a channel into an empty mould; when the bronze cools (it darkens over 3 seconds), it becomes a new platform that throws shade.
> 4. **The furnace heart.** Cross the main furnace floor on its rhythm. At full flare there is no shade anywhere for one second: Step, wait, Strike, Step. **Test** of all three powers on rhythm.
> 5. **Optional: the bell tower.** Ponni's bell, the coronation bell, hangs frozen mid-swing in the casting tower. Climb to it for a shadow-memory: *"Ponni, bell-caster. She never heard it ring."*

---

**INT. ABANDONED CASTER'S SHED. LATER. (THE CAMPFIRE)**

A small shed off the pouring floor. A forge banked low, the only fire in Kanchi that isn't frozen, because Kayal lit it herself with flint. It throws a warm, moving light, and the moving light makes moving shadows, which Nizhal finds hilarious and keeps playing with.

ILAN and KAYAL sit on either side of a **pallanguzhi** board they found on the shelf: a hinged wooden board with two rows of seven cups, and a bag of cowrie shells.

> **KAYAL**
> Do you know how to play?

> **ILAN**
> Everyone knows how to play.

> **KAYAL**
> Then you'll lose quickly.

> **GAMEPLAY: PALLANGUZHI (optional, about 5 min, best of 3 against Kayal)**
>
> Each cup starts with 5 shells. Take all the shells from one of your cups and sow them one by one into the following cups, anticlockwise. When the last shell lands, pick up the shells in the *next* cup and keep sowing. If the next cup is empty, take the shells in the cup after it as your capture, and your turn ends. Kayal plays well; she was taught by her father on long temple nights.
>
> The conversation plays whatever happens on the board. A line triggers on each capture.

They play. The dialogue comes slowly, between moves.

> **ILAN**
> I didn't open the door to save the coronation.

> **KAYAL**
> I know.

> **ILAN**
> I wanted my name carved next to the kings. Like my father never got.

> **ILAN**
> Everyone in Kanchi walks past his wall. Nobody knows who built it.

Kayal sows shells for a while without answering.

> **KAYAL**
> When it happened, I was in the tank alone. In the dark.

> **KAYAL**
> I'm the only one who knows the old lore now. My father's lore.

> **KAYAL**
> I'm scared I'll get it wrong. That I only ever knew it because he did.

> **ILAN**
> You read every inscription in that tank.

> **KAYAL**
> He made me.

> **ILAN**
> You still read them.

She looks at him properly for the first time. Then she captures six of his shells.

> **KAYAL**
> You lose.

Behind them, NIZHAL sits in the dark corner of the shed, flat against the wall, perfectly still. Only his eyes move between them.

---

**INT. THE OLD FORGE (MAYAN'S FORGE). NIGHT, IN THE DARK CHAMBERS.**

Below the foundry, a sealed forge older than the city. On the walls, faded red-ochre paintings of a man casting a black disc. KAYAL holds a lamp up to them. One of the shadows on the wall doesn't move when the lamp moves.

It peels off the wall: a **shadow-memory of MAYAN**, the architect, three hundred years dead. A tall old man made of shadow, with his sleeves rolled up.

> **MAYAN (shadow-memory)**
> *(not to them; a memory of something he once said)*
> I built a glass that keeps every shadow Kanchi casts.

> **MAYAN**
> So the city never forgets anyone. Not the potter. Not the water-carrier.

> **MAYAN**
> But a glass that keeps everything must be closed by someone who gives up something.

> **MAYAN**
> Whoever mends it gives it their own shadow. And the city forgets them.

> **MAYAN**
> That is the price. I paid it once. No one remembers who I was.

> **MAYAN**
> They call me Mayan. It is not my name.

The memory sinks back into the wall. Silence.

ILAN turns slowly to KAYAL.

> **ILAN**
> You knew.

> **KAYAL**
> The carving in the tank.

> **ILAN**
> You *knew*, and you let me...

> **KAYAL**
> I let you what? You broke it. Someone has to mend it.

> **ILAN**
> So it might as well be the fool who broke it?

> **KAYAL**
> *(furious and wet-eyed)*
> I didn't know how to tell you you'd vanish. I didn't know you yet.

> **KAYAL**
> I'm sorry. I'm sorry I didn't tell you.

They stand on opposite sides of the forge. Their first real fight. Neither moves.

---

**EXT. THE WAR YARD. LATER.**

A huge open casting yard. In its centre, half-buried in its casting pit, its mould only half broken away, is **THE BRONZE COLOSSUS**: a thirty-foot dwarapala commissioned for the palace gate for the coronation. One fang bared, one hand raised in warning, a club the size of a palm tree. Its eyes light violet. It pulls itself out of the pit.

> **BOSS: THE BRONZE COLOSSUS (climbed like a level, 3 phases)**
>
> - **Phase 1: the feet.** It stomps across the yard, and the shadow of its raised foot is the only shade for twenty metres. Step into the shadow of the raised foot and Strike the ankle seal (the casting seam). Both ankles: it drops to one knee.
> - **Phase 2: the arm.** Climb the club arm. Anchor to hold a loose armour plate open while Kayal shoots its elbow joint; then Strike through the gap.
> - **Phase 3: the furnace.** On its shoulders, the core glows in its chest. When the main furnace behind it flares, its own body shades its chest from the glow for one second. Step in and Strike the core.

The Colossus falls forward, slowly, into its own casting pit, raising a dust cloud over the whole quarter. The shard rises out of its chest and floats to Ilan.

---

**EXT. THE WAR YARD. DUSK LIGHT FROM THE FURNACES.**

ILAN holds the shard. KAYAL stands apart.

> **ILAN**
> *(not looking at her)*
> I'll still do it. I'll mend it.

> **KAYAL**
> *(quietly)*
> I know.

She walks away to look for the next path. ILAN turns the shard over in his fingers.

> **ILAN**
> *(barely moving his lips, to Nizhal)*
> Find me another way.

NIZHAL leans close to his ear. For the first time his eyes flicker **red**, then violet again.

> **NIZHAL**
> I'll look. I promise.

The shard pulses, and the sound of a temple bell rings once, from a long time ago.

## Lore inscriptions

1. *"The wax goes, the bronze stays. So too the body and the name."* (wax room)
2. *"Clay from the Vegavathi, wax from the hill bees, resin from the kungiliyam tree."* (clay pit; the real lost-wax recipe)
3. *"Five metals in the god: copper, tin, gold, silver, lead. Panchaloha."* (pouring floor)
4. *"Guard the gate. Warn the proud."* (on the Colossus's plinth; dwarapalas raise a hand in warning)
5. *"I paid. No one remembers. That is the payment."* (Mayan's forge, in red ochre)

## Accuracy notes

- **Chola bronze casting** used the lost-wax method: a beeswax model mixed with kungiliyam resin, coated in fine river clay, heated so the wax runs out, then filled with molten metal. Swamimalai still works this way today. Show wax models, clay-coated moulds and broken-open moulds.
- **Dwarapalas** (door-guardians) are standard at Chola temple gateways: one raised hand in a warning gesture, a club, sometimes a fang. They are traditionally stone; a 30-foot bronze one is our fiction, and the dialogue says it was a special commission.
- **Pallanguzhi** is a traditional Tamil mancala game with 14 cups and cowrie shells or tamarind seeds. The rules described are a standard variant.
